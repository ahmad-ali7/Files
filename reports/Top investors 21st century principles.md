# Evidence shrinks the legends, keeps the lessons

Judged on documents rather than reputation, the ten most important investors of 2000–2026 are Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger (Berkshire Hathaway), Nick Sleep and Qais Zakaria (Nomad), Howard Marks (Oaktree), David Swensen (Yale), Bill Ackman (Pershing Square), Terry Smith (Fundsmith), Jim Simons (Renaissance Medallion), Stanley Druckenmiller (Duquesne), Seth Klarman (Baupost) and Ray Dalio (Bridgewater). Only six of them have a 21st-century record that can be checked against audited letters, SEC filings or official releases and that beat a fair benchmark after fees: Berkshire, Nomad, Oaktree, Yale, Pershing and Fundsmith. Even in those six, the edge was concentrated in a few years and shrank as assets grew. **Berkshire's stock beat the S&P 500 by about 2.4 points a year over 2000–2025, but it trailed the index slightly from 2010 to 2025 (13.5% vs 14.1% a year)**. Over the same stretch, Fundsmith lagged its index for five straight years, Baupost earned about 4% a year after 2014, and Bridgewater's flagship earned under 3% a year from 2012 to 2024. The most famous figures, such as Medallion's 66%, Nomad's 921% and Duquesne's "30 years, no losses", turn out to be arithmetic averages, pre-fee numbers or self-reported. Read against that evidence, the principles that hold up are mostly the unglamorous ones. Low costs, broad diversification, staying invested, and avoiding leverage and forced selling have strong evidence behind them. Buying quality at sensible prices and holding cash to deploy in a crisis have real but intermittent support. Concentrated conviction bets and copying star managers are largely stories told about survivors. For an investor in Pakistan, inflation and the rupee dominate the result. My own calculation puts the KSE-100 at **about 14% a year in rupees but only about 5% a year in dollars** between its 2008 and 2025 peaks, so a low-cost, Shariah-compliant index core held through the cycle, measured in real dollar terms, beats any imitation of a celebrity portfolio. This is research, not financial advice.

## Ten investors chosen by verifiable record, not fame

A "top 10" list says as much about its filters as about the investors, so the filters here are explicit. Each candidate was scored on five tests. The first is a **record inside 2000–2026**: a 1980s or 1990s reputation does not count. The second is **verification quality**: audited letters, SEC filings and official university releases rank above press leaks, and press leaks rank above self-report. The third is **performance net of fees against a fair benchmark**. The fourth is **scale and influence**, meaning the size of the capital and how far their methods spread. The fifth is a **body of their own writing** (letters, memos, books or full transcripts), because the user asked for principles in the investors' own words. Two partnerships are counted as one slot because they ran one vehicle: Buffett with Munger, and Sleep with Zakaria. The ten then fall into three tiers. These are tiers of evidence, not of talent.

The tiering shows something a raw ranking would hide. On raw returns Medallion would be first by a wide margin. But nobody outside Renaissance could invest in it, and its figures trace to a single book and press leaks ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)). Dalio earns a slot on influence and absolute dollars, not percentages. At the end of 2025 LCH ranked Bridgewater **third all-time in net gains at $79.1bn**, behind Citadel and D.E. Shaw ([Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/top-20-hedge-funds-earn-115b-in-2025/)). The brief's description of Bridgewater as "flat 2012–2024" slightly understates it: press reports put the figure at **under 3% a year**, which is weak but not zero ([Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium)).

Several prominent names were left out deliberately, and the reasons are themselves findings. **Li Lu, Rakesh Jhunjhunwala and Mohnish Pabrai** are covered in the emerging-markets section rather than the ten. Li Lu's ~26–30% figures are unaudited and the firm publishes nothing ([Wikipedia: Himalaya Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital)). Jhunjhunwala left a net worth, not a return series. Pabrai's own self-reported figures conflict, ranging from 35.3% to about 12% a year depending on the window ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/2010/04/10/pabrai-dollar-cost-average-intelligent-investing-entrepreneur.html); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/04/mohnish-pabrai-still-finding-undervalued-opportunities-today)). **Peter Thiel, Sequoia and Masayoshi Son** produced the century's largest single multiples. Thiel's $500,000 in Facebook in 2004 returned over $1bn in 2012 sales ([CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2012/08/20/technology/facebook-peter-thiel/)), and Son's $20m in Alibaba became a stake worth more than $100bn ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/alibaba-shares-tumble-after-softbank-reportedly-sells-most-of-its-stake.html)). These are deal-level outliers in a power-law asset class, not portfolio records. In Kauffman's 20-year review of about 100 venture funds, only 20 beat public markets by the 3%+ a year needed to justify illiquidity ([Kauffman](https://kauffman.org/reports/we-have-met-the-enemy-and-he-is-us)), and SoftBank's Vision Fund lost about $14bn on WeWork alone ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/02/07/softbank-nets-20-billion-on-arm-earnings-exceeding-losses-in-wework.html)).

Six more are treated as **cautionary cases**, because a famous peak was followed by reversal. John Paulson converted to a family office in 2020 after his subprime triumph faded ([Zuckerman](https://www.gregoryzuckerman.com/2020/08/05/hedge-fund-star-john-paulson-calls-it-quits/)). Bill Miller's 15-year streak ended, and Legg Mason Value Trust then fell about 55% in 2008 against −37% for the S&P 500 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/mutual-funds/2009/01/06/the-worst-mutual-funds-of-2008.aspx)). In ARKK, investors lost far more than the fund did because money arrived at the top ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/markets/arkk-an-object-lesson-how-not-invest)). Tiger Global's hedge fund fell 56% in 2022 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b8x4y67blyqdwz/hedge-funds-lost-more-than-200-billion-last-year)). Archegos's leveraged swaps cost banks over $10bn, and Bill Hwang received 18 years ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2024/11/20/archegoss-bill-hwang-sentenced-to-18-years-in-prison-for-massive-us-fraud)). Michael Burry deregistered Scion in November 2025, which ended public tracking of his record ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/private-equity/michael-burrys-scion-hedge-fund-deregistered-sec-filing-shows)). One great call, whether Paulson's 2007 short or Burry's 2008 bet, proved a poor guide to what came next.

## The ten records, read from their own documents

Each profile below follows the same template. ### Buffett and Munger: a 2.4-point lead, all of it earned in 2000–2009

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Period / vehicle | 2000–2025 (Buffett CEO to end-2025; Greg Abel CEO from 1 Jan 2026). Berkshire Hathaway, a permanent-capital holding company funded by insurance float of $176bn at end-2025 ([2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)). Munger died 28 Nov 2023 ([2023 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf)). |
| Strategy | Whole-company acquisitions of capital-heavy US businesses; crisis-time structured deals (high-coupon preferred plus warrants); a concentrated equity book; large cash reserve. |
| Key trades | **2008**: $14.5bn of high-yield securities from Goldman Sachs ($5bn, 10%), GE and Wrigley, each with an equity "bonus" ([2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)). Goldman redeemed for $5.5bn plus dividends in April 2011 ([Goldman](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2011/redeem-stock)). **2008** BYD: 225m shares for $232m, worth $5.9bn at end-2020 ([2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf)), fully exited by 2025 [P] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/21/buffett-munger-byd-exits-stake.html)). **2010** BNSF: $22bn cash plus 6% share dilution, to raise "normal" earning power by nearly 40% ([2010 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2010ltr.pdf)). **2011** Bank of America: $5bn 6% preferred with warrants on 700m shares at $7.14 ([2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf)), worth $20.7bn at end-2017 ([2017 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2017ltr.pdf)). **2016–18** Apple: about 1bn shares for $36bn ([2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf)), trimmed to a $6.3bn cost basis / $62bn value by end-2025. **From 4 Jul 2019** five Japanese trading houses, financed with yen debt: cost $15.4bn, value $35.4bn at end-2025 ([2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)). **2025** OxyChem for $9.7bn [P] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/02/warren-buffett-berkshire-occidental-oxychem.html)). **2026** $10bn Alphabet private placement ([Alphabet 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000119312526257724/d83560dex991.htm)). |
| Returns | Market value per share **10.5%/yr vs S&P 500 TR 8.1%/yr, 2000–2025** [C]; **5.9% vs −0.9% in 2000–09**; **13.5% vs 14.1% in 2010–25** [C], all from the annual table in the [2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf). No fees, so effectively net. The 1965–2025 record is 19.7% vs 10.5% [V]. |
| Signature mistakes | ConocoPhillips near the 2008 oil peak ("cost Berkshire several billion dollars"); two Irish banks, down 89% ([2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)). About $2bn of Energy Future Holdings bonds ([2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf)). Precision Castparts, $32bn+, with an $11bn write-down: "I paid too much" ([2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf)). Kraft Heinz, which Abel calls "well short of adequate" ([2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)). Munger called Alibaba "one of the worst mistakes I ever made" ([DJCO 2023](https://steadycompounding.com/transcript/djco23/)). |
| Core principles (quotes) | "Price is what you pay; value is what you get… I like buying quality merchandise when it is marked down" ([2008](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)). "We will never become dependent on the kindness of strangers" ([2009](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)). "Huge sums forge their own anchor" ([2009](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)). "The cardinal sin is delaying the correction of mistakes" ([2024](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf)). Munger: "wonderful businesses purchased at fair prices" over "fair businesses at wonderful prices" ([2023](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf)). |

Berkshire's own table supports the brief's premise and sharpens it. Berkshire beat the S&P in 16 of 26 years. The cumulative lead, however, came from **defence in 2000–2002 and 2008**, which matches Buffett's own line that "our defense has been better than our offense" ([2009 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)). In the 2010 letter Buffett hoped to "average several points better than the S&P" ([2010 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2010ltr.pdf)). That hope was not met over the following 16 years. The best trades were **liquidity sold on Berkshire's own terms** in panics (Goldman, Bank of America, Occidental). The worst were **full-price acquisitions** (Precision Castparts, Kraft Heinz). Academic work strips away much of the mystique. "Buffett's Alpha" finds that Berkshire's excess return becomes statistically insignificant once low-beta (BAB) and quality (QMJ) factors are controlled for, and that it was amplified by about **1.7x leverage from cheap insurance float** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3197185)). Individuals can reproduce the factor tilt. They cannot reproduce the float.

### Sleep and Zakaria: the best audited net record, and it is 18.4%, not 921%

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Period / vehicle | 10 Sep 2001 – 2014. Nomad Investment Partnership, a small global long-only partnership. Marathon was general partner until 2006, then Sleep, Zakaria & Co ([2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)). |
| Strategy | Phase 1 (2001–03): firms "trading at around half of their real business value", heavily in Southeast Asia (32.8%), with about 28% cash ([2001 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e8204e94728ecc4b13995_2001%20Nomad%20Interim%20Letter.pdf)). Phase 2: a concentrated book of **"scale economies shared"** compounders, mainly Amazon, Costco and Berkshire. |
| Key trades | Invested quickly after 9/11, having started on 10 Sep 2001 ([2001 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e8204e94728ecc4b13995_2001%20Nomad%20Interim%20Letter.pdf)). Zimbabwe cement, brewery and engineering stakes were marked to zero during hyperinflation, then sold in 2013 at 3–8x in USD ([2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)). |
| Returns | **+921.1% cumulative and 20.8%/yr before performance fees; 18.4%/yr after; MSCI World +116.9% and 6.5%/yr** (to 31 Dec 2013). Figures in the letter are "unaudited", but the performance series was audited by E&Y and later Grant Thornton [V] ([2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)). 2008: −45.3% vs −40.7%; 2009: +71.5%. |
| Fees | Cost-reimbursement management fee of about 0.10% a year, plus a refundable, hurdle-based performance fee [V]. The exact terms (reportedly 20% above 6%) are [P]. |
| Signature mistakes | Stagecoach ("denial… and ego"); Monsanto sold after an analytical error; "the biggest error an investor can make is the sale of a Wal-Mart or a Microsoft" ([Massive Moats](https://massivemoats.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-analysis-into-the-nomad)). Zimbabwe was "not one we would repeat in a hurry" ([2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)). |
| Core principles (quotes) | "Most companies pursue scale efficiencies, but few share them" (2004); "the decision not to do something is still an active decision" (2012) ([Massive Moats](https://massivemoats.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-analysis-into-the-nomad)). Returns are "the capitalisation of the success of the firms in which we have invested (minus our fees!)", and Nomad held positions long enough to "write around twenty letters during the life of the average investment" ([2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)). |

Nomad has the cleanest evidence of genuine excess return in this group: **nearly 12 points a year above MSCI World, net, over 12 years**. Two features make it hard to generalise from. First, the fund was small. Second, about half of its compounding came in **2009–2013 (+404% over five years)**, after a 2008 loss worse than the index. The letters show that much of the result came from a handful of names (Amazon, Costco, Berkshire, AirAsia), which is the Bessembinder skew discussed below. The fee design is the most transferable innovation: costs passed through at about 10 basis points, and a performance fee that could be clawed back.

### Marks: a $10.9bn crisis fund that netted 16.7%, not the early 31.5%

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Period / vehicle | Oaktree Capital, co-founded in 1995 (pre-period). The key 21st-century vehicle is **OCM Opportunities Fund VIIb**: $10.94bn committed, investing May 2008 – May 2011 ([Oaktree FY2015 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403528/000140352816000045/a201510-kdoc.htm)). Brookfield bought 62% for about $4.7bn in 2019 ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/brookfield-buys-oaktree-to-build-an-alternatives-giant/78581)) and agreed to buy the remaining ~26% for about $3bn in Oct 2025 ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/esg/brookfield-to-acquire-remaining-oaktree-stake-for-3-billion-1)). |
| Strategy | Distressed debt and credit cycle investing: raise money while prices are high, hold commitments uncalled, then deploy at maximum pessimism. |
| Key trades | Warned in Feb 2007 of a "race to the bottom" in credit standards, citing covenant erosion and PIK toggles ([memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-02-14-the-race-to-the-bottom.pdf)). After Lehman, deployed **about $450–500m+ a week for about 15 weeks, roughly $6–7bn** [P; the exact total is unconfirmed] ([Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/rob-magazine/invest-like-a-legend-howard-marks/article37716588/)). In Dec 2022 called a "sea change" to a "full-return world" for credit ([Sea Change](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change)). |
| Returns | VIIb as of 31 Dec 2015: **22.0% gross / 16.7% net IRR, 2.0x**, about $17.3bn distributed on $9.84bn drawn [V, SEC filing; the column mapping should be re-checked] ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403528/000140352816000045/a201510-kdoc.htm)). The earlier "31.5% net IRR to end-2009" came from an unnamed investor, on interim marks [P] ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgc08ca1yjgus3sms5c/portfolio/howard-marks-the-distressed-debt-king)). |
| Signature mistakes | By his own account, Oaktree spent 2009–2019 "in the wilderness" and cut the size of the successor fund in half ([Sea Change](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change)). There was too little distress to deploy into. |
| Core principles (quotes) | "Skepticism and pessimism aren't synonymous… it also calls for optimism when pessimism is excessive"; "Sure, the negative story may turn out to be true, but certainly it's priced into the market… I buy!" ([Limits to Negativism, Oct 2008](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-10-15-the-limits-to-negativism.pdf)). Quoting Buffett: "The less prudence with which others conduct their affairs, the greater the prudence with which we should conduct our own affairs" ([Race to the Bottom](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-02-14-the-race-to-the-bottom.pdf)). |

Marks's distinctive contribution is a **process that is documented in advance**. The warning came in February 2007, the buy rationale in October 2008, and the redeployment call in December 2022, each in a dated memo. That makes his method unusually checkable. The headline IRR shrank by almost half between the first leak and the SEC filing, which is a good lesson in how interim private-fund marks flatter results. His "second-level thinking" formulation is widely quoted, but the research could not verify the exact wording against *The Most Important Thing*, so it is paraphrased here and not quoted.

### Swensen: the endowment model's architect told individuals not to copy it

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Period / vehicle | CIO of the Yale endowment from 1985 until his death in May 2021. The century window is FY2001–FY2021. Matt Mendelsohn has been CIO since. |
| Strategy | Equity-oriented and heavily diversified into illiquid alternatives (private equity, venture, real assets, absolute return), run by top-quartile external managers ([Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2021/10/22/david-swensens-coda)). |
| Key trades | The allocation itself was the trade. No single-deal sizes are documented in the notes. After Swensen, Yale ran its first secondary sale of private-equity stakes in 2025, about $2.5bn ("Project Gatsby"), though early reports said up to $6bn [P] ([Advisor Perspectives/Bloomberg](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2025/06/09/yales-2-5-billion-private-equity-sale-tests-vaunted-endowment-model)). |
| Returns | **11.3%/yr over the 20 years to June 2021 vs 9.1% domestic stocks, 4.6% bonds, 7.7% average endowment**. FY2021 was +40.2% net [V] ([Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2021/10/14/yale-endowment-earns-402-investment-return-fiscal-2021)). Full tenure: 13.7%/yr, 3.4 points a year above the average endowment [V] ([Yale News coda](https://news.yale.edu/2021/10/22/david-swensens-coda)). After Swensen: FY2023 1.8% ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/yales-41-4-billion-fund-posts-second-lowest-ivy-league-return)); FY2024 5.7% ([Yale](https://news.yale.edu/2024/10/25/yale-reports-investment-return-fiscal-2024)); FY2025 11.1%, "trailed public indices" ([Yale](https://news.yale.edu/2025/10/24/yale-reports-investment-return-fiscal-2025)); **FY2026 26.3%**, 10-year 11.7%/yr, 2.7 points a year above a 70/30 portfolio ([Yale](https://news.yale.edu/2026/10/27/yale-reports-investment-return-fiscal-2026)). In FY2026 Yale trailed Penn (27.4%) and Harvard (27.1%) ([ai-CIO](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/yale-endowment-delivers-26-3-investment-return-for-fy-2026/)). |
| Signature mistakes | No documented single mistake by Swensen himself. The model's weakness showed after him: private-heavy portfolios lagged in FY2023–25, and the 2025 forced secondary sale exposed the cost of illiquidity. |
| Core principles | In *Unconventional Success* (2005) he told individuals to hold broadly diversified, low-cost index funds from not-for-profit providers such as Vanguard and TIAA-CREF, because "at least 99 percent of mutual funds aren't going to beat the overall market" (NPR's summary) ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2006/10/05/6204218/david-swensens-advice-for-the-individual-investor)). His widely reproduced model portfolio (30% US stocks, 15% developed international, 5% EM, 20% REITs, 15% Treasuries, 15% TIPS) is [P] and was not checked against the book ([StockCharts](https://stockcharts.com:443/articles/muscular-investing/2019/07/unconventional-success-portfolio.html)). |

Swensen's record is official, long and positive. It is also the strongest piece of evidence that **institutional edges do not transfer to individuals**, because the person who built the model said so himself. The edge depended on access to top-quartile managers, a perpetual horizon and a large staff. FY2026's 26.3% weakens the "Yale model is dead" story of 2023–25, but it does not erase it.

## What survivorship, fees and size do to the legends

Set side by side, the ten records share five regularities that matter more than any individual's tactics.

The first is that **edge decays with size, and the investors themselves said so**. Buffett wrote in 2009 that "our future advantage, if any, will be a small fraction of our historical edge" ([2009 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)), and the 2010–2025 data proved him right. Medallion avoided decay only by capping itself at about $10bn and paying out profits. Baupost, Fundsmith and Bridgewater all lost their excess return as assets peaked. Nomad closed while small. The lesson for selecting managers is that the record which attracts money is usually the record the larger fund cannot repeat.

The second is that **gross vs net is not a footnote**. Medallion's gross-to-net gap averaged about 27 points a year ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=33535)). Nomad's was 2.4 points on a fee structure investors considered generous ([2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)). An LCH-linked study found that **hedge fund investors lost over half of their gross gains to fees** ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/hedge-fund-investors-have-lost-over-half-their-gains-to-fees-finds-study/258986)). Buffett's ten-year bet was at heart a fee test. Fund-of-funds charged about 2.5% a year in fixed fees, and **all five trailed the index in each of the nine years after 2008**; the S&P index fund made 125.8% against 2.8–87.7% ([2017 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2017ltr.pdf)).

The third is that **most legendary numbers are unaudited**. Only Berkshire, Nomad, Oaktree's SEC-filed funds, Yale, Pershing and Fundsmith publish figures that can be checked. Medallion, Duquesne, Baupost, Bridgewater, Himalaya and Pabrai's funds are known through books, leaks or self-report, and where several sources exist they conflict.

The fourth is **survivorship, which is built into any list like this one**. In S&P's Year-End 2025 data, only **36.5% of the 2,447 US domestic equity funds alive 20 years earlier still existed**. Over 20 years, **95.0% of all domestic funds and 92.9% of large-cap funds underperformed** their benchmarks, and about 97–98% did so on a risk-adjusted basis ([SPIVA U.S. YE2025](https://www.ariesadvisors.com/files/spiva-us-year-end-2025.pdf)). Persistence is weak. In most domestic categories, **no top-quartile fund from 2021 was still top-quartile at end-2025** ([SPIVA Persistence YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/persistence-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf)). The academic summary is that aggregate active net alpha is negative by about the amount of fees, with a debated sliver of pre-fee skill in the tails ([Fama & French 2010](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/bespeneckbo/default/AFA611-Eckbo%20web%20site/AFA611-S8C-FamaFrench-LuckvSkill-JF10.pdf); [Harvey & Liu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P151_Luck_versus_skill.pdf)). Stock returns are themselves extremely skewed. **About 4% of US listed companies created all net wealth above T-bills from 1926 to 2016**, and most stocks underperformed T-bills ([Bessembinder 2018](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/sites/default/files/2021-10/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills.pdf)). That skew is why concentrated portfolios usually lag, and why the few that caught Amazon, Apple or Titan become the stories that get told.

The fifth is that **investors in star funds earn less than the funds themselves**. Morningstar's dollar-weighted study puts the average gap at about **1.2 points a year over the decade to 2024**, rising to about 2 points in the most volatile funds and close to zero in target-date funds ([Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025)). ARKK is the extreme case: one Morningstar update put the three-year dollar-weighted loss at about 28% a year, more than double the fund's own reported loss ([Morningstar SG](https://sg.morningstar.com/sg/news/236131/why-arkk-investors-are-still-underwater.aspx)). Fundsmith's money peaked at about £29bn just before five years of underperformance, and PSUS investors were down about 21% within months of the IPO.

"Survivorship story" means the principle is told by winners and refuted or untested in the population that includes the losers.

Do not hold large discretionary cash waiting for a crash. |
| Admit and fix mistakes fast (Buffett, Smith, Druckenmiller) | **Moderate** (qualitative) | Documented write-downs at PCC, Kraft Heinz and Novo; selling losers is consistent with momentum research | Stock-pickers | Write a one-paragraph thesis for every stock bought and sell when the thesis (not the price) breaks. |
| Uncorrelated diversification across assets (Dalio) | **Moderate** | All Weather +20.4% in 2025; Pure Alpha <3%/yr 2012–24 | Individuals (simple multi-asset); leveraged versions for professionals | Hold equities, high-grade bonds and an inflation hedge suited to your currency, without leverage. |
| Margin of safety in valuation (Klarman, Marks, Li Lu) | **Moderate** as process, **weak** as a timing tool | Value's long drawdown, which even proponents accept ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Is-Systematic-Value-Investing-Dead)) | Stock-pickers | Demand a larger discount where governance, currency or liquidity risk is higher. |
| Concentrate and "bet big" (Druckenmiller, Ackman, Munger, Pabrai) | **Survivorship story** for individuals | Bessembinder skew; Ackman 2011–18 at 1.5%/yr; Nomad −45% and Pabrai −60% in 2008; Tiger, ARKK | Professionals with an edge and a mandate | Cap any single stock at 5–10% of your liquid wealth, unless it is your own business. |
| Follow or clone star managers and 13Fs | **Survivorship story** | Persistence near zero ([SPIVA Persistence](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/persistence-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf)); 13F excludes macro books and arrives about 45 days late | Nobody, as a strategy | Use letters for ideas and education, never as a buy list. |
| Venture and private markets for outsized returns (Thiel, Sequoia, Swensen's Yale) | **Weak** for individuals | Kauffman: 20 of 100 funds earned the premium; about 65% of deals return below cost [P] ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/the-power-law-in-vc-returns-why-one-investment-defines-a-fund)); Swensen's own advice | Institutions with top-quartile access | Do not pay for illiquidity premia you cannot verify. |
| Macro and liquidity-driven timing (Druckenmiller, Dalio) | **Weak** for individuals | Druckenmiller's 2000 loss; Pure Alpha's 2012–24 record; Klarman's 2010 worry did not pay | Professionals only | Use macro conditions to set expectations and savings rates, not to move in and out of markets. |

The ranking points to a counterintuitive conclusion. **The principles with the strongest evidence are the ones the ten recommend for other people**, such as Buffett's index-fund advice and Swensen's not-for-profit index funds. The ones they used themselves (concentration, crisis leverage via float or structured deals, private access, short-horizon trading) depend on capital structures and access that individuals lack. The ten's habits that do transfer are temperament rather than tactics: patience, refusing leverage, admitting errors, and buying when others are forced sellers. For an individual, rebalancing is the mechanical way to be the buyer in a panic without having to time it.

## Applying the principles in Pakistan and emerging markets

The emerging-market investors excluded from the ten add three points the US-centred records cannot. Rakesh Jhunjhunwala built most of his roughly **$5.8bn fortune** ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2022/08/14/indias-warren-buffet-started-trading-with-rs-5000-has-net-worth-over-dollar58-billion.html)) from one long hold. He bought Titan around 2002–03 at roughly Rs 3 split-adjusted [P] ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/the-big-bull-of-the-indian-stock-market-rakesh-jhunjhunwalas-legacy)) and owned **a 5.05% stake worth more than Rs 11,000 crore** at his death in 2022 [V via exchange data] ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/podcast/current-affairs/how-did-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-make-his-billions-122081600056_1.html)). That outcome rode on two decades of Indian growth and a relatively stable currency. Mohnish Pabrai's own January 2009 letter reports the funds "off by about 60% in 2008", and shows a $100,000 investment made in 1999 compounding at only about 10.9% a year to end-2008 ([Pabrai letter](https://snowballing-co.s3.amazonaws.com/media/l_010109.pdf)). His later move into India, Turkey and South Korea produced results commentators call mixed ([Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/04/mohnish-pabrai-still-finding-undervalued-opportunities-today)). Li Lu's principles of circle of competence, margin of safety and "fish where the fish are" ([Marcellus](https://marcellus.in/story/long-read-global-value-investing-in-our-era/)) travel well. His BYD success, bought near the 2002 IPO, is the same power-law pattern, and his returns of about 26% a year from 1998 to 2010 are unaudited ([Wikipedia: Himalaya Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital)).

Pakistan's market makes **currency and inflation the first-order variables**. The KSE-100 peaked at **15,676 on 18 April 2008** at about Rs 62 to the dollar ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSE_100_Index)) and hit a record **170,741 on 15 December 2025** after the SBP cut its policy rate to 10.5% ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/12/15/kse-100-hits-record-170741-as-sbp-rate-cut-lifts-sentiment)). On my price-basis arithmetic, using about Rs 280 to the dollar at end-2025, that is **about 14.4% a year in rupees but about 5.1% a year in dollars** over 17.7 years. Even that flatters the result, because it ends on a record high. Over 2022–23 the index was roughly flat in rupees while the rupee fell from about 178 to over 280 per dollar, which halved its dollar value ([Kahloon](https://kahloon.substack.com/p/the-kse-100-crash-you-could-have)). CPI peaked at **38.0% in May 2023**, the highest since records began in 1965 ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40245453)). The real rupee return over the full period is therefore a fraction of the nominal figure, but no sourced cumulative CPI series was available, so I do not put a number on it.

The market is also prone to violent swings and to intervention. In August 2008 the exchange **froze prices with a floor** at 9,144.93 for months ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/318671)). MSCI upgraded Pakistan to Emerging in 2017 and then **downgraded it to Frontier in 2021** for shrinking size and liquidity ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2021/09/08/msci-downgrades-pakistan-to-frontier-markets)). The index fell about 15% in one week in March 2020 ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2181227/2-weekly-review-kse-100-dives-15-records-biggest-weekly-losts-since-2008-business/)). It then gained **203% in rupees over FY24–FY25** as rates fell from 22% ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40370061/kse-100-beats-us-india-amp-germany-to-emerge-among-top-global-performers-in-fy25)), before dropping **9.57% in a single day on 2 March 2026** ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/pakistans-kse-100-freefalls-9-in-worst-rout-since-2008-trading-resumes-after-brief-halt)) and returning −14.6% in dollars in January–March 2026 ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2553733/psx-tops-asian-markets-with-60-return)). The behaviour-gap lesson applies with full force here: investors who left after the flat, currency-crushed 2022–23 missed a doubling-plus.

No SPIVA scorecard exists for Pakistan. The closest evidence points the same way as the US data: **about 73% of Indian large-cap funds and 82% of mid/small-cap funds trailed their benchmarks over 10 years** [P, readings vary] ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/35917-73-of-indian-large-cap-and-82-of-mid-and-small-cap-funds-have-underperformed-sp-india-benchmarks-over-a-10-year-period)), and **87.7% of US-domiciled EM funds lagged over 10 years** ([SPIVA](https://www.ariesadvisors.com/files/spiva-us-year-end-2025.pdf)). The local complication is cost. The Shariah-compliant **Meezan Pakistan ETF** reported a total expense ratio of about **0.94–0.95% in its September 2026 fund manager report**, having ranged from 0.9% to 1.9% over 2024–26 ([PSX disclosure](https://dps.psx.com.pk/download/document/285767.pdf)). The KSE Meezan Index Fund (tracking KMI-30) is listed by one aggregator at a **2.28% TER plus a 2% front-end load**, which may be out of date ([IFN Investor](https://ifninvestor.com/fund/kse-meezan-index-fund/)). Passive is cheaper than active in Pakistan, but not cheap, so the TER difference between products matters a great deal.

| Rule for a Pakistani / EM individual investor | Rationale and evidence |
|---|---|

| **Diversify currency exposure** for part of long-term savings, through whatever legally permitted foreign-currency or global-index channels you can access | The 2022–23 halving in USD terms happened with a flat index ([Kahloon](https://kahloon.substack.com/p/the-kse-100-crash-you-could-have)). Specific channels were not researched and should be checked with SECP and SBP rules |
| **Never use margin or leverage** on PSX | Price floor and freeze in 2008, a 15% weekly fall in 2020, a 9.57% one-day fall in 2026 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/318671); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/pakistans-kse-100-freefalls-9-in-worst-rout-since-2008-trading-resumes-after-brief-halt)) |
| Invest monthly and **rebalance on schedule**; do not exit after a bad year | FY24–25 +203% followed the flat 2022–23 period ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40370061/kse-100-beats-us-india-amp-germany-to-emerge-among-top-global-performers-in-fy25)) |
| If you pick stocks, stay inside your **circle of competence**, demand a margin of safety that covers FX and governance risk, and cap single names at 5–10% | Li Lu's principles ([Marcellus](https://marcellus.in/story/long-read-global-value-investing-in-our-era/)); Pabrai's −60% year ([letter](https://snowballing-co.s3.amazonaws.com/media/l_010109.pdf)) |
| Do not copy Jhunjhunwala-style concentration on the assumption that Pakistan will follow India's path | His Titan result depended on India's macro stability, a premise Pakistan has not yet met; the KSE-100 is itself concentrated in rate- and energy-sensitive sectors (inference, weights not sourced) |

## Conclusion

Reading the records from primary documents changes the question. It is less "how did the greatest investors win?" and more "why do their best results come early, in crises, and while they were small?" The answer is the same mechanism every time: excess returns attract capital, capital erodes the edge, and the public arrives near the peak. That is why the ten's most durable contribution is the advice several of them gave to everyone else (index cheaply, never borrow, hold through panics, admit errors) rather than the strategies they ran. The century's verified data is consistent: costs, diversification and behaviour explain most of what an individual can control, and the famous concentrated bets are mostly visible because their owners survived.

For a Pakistani investor the same logic applies with a currency adjustment. The question that matters is not which legend to imitate. It is how to own the broad market cheaply in Shariah-compliant form, measure it in real dollars, keep enough liquidity never to be a forced seller during a 9% down day, and diversify some savings beyond the rupee. The investors who did best on PSX's 2023–25 doubling were those who were already invested and stayed. That is a lesson Buffett, Sleep and Marks would recognise, and one that needs no edge to execute.
