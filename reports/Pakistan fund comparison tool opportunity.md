# Build Pakistan's fund scorecard, sell data first

**Verdict: a conditional go, but only as a lean, bootstrapped data-and-research business. Do not launch it as a consumer app or a venture-scale platform.** Start with a free, full-market, fee-transparent comparison and a published active-vs-index scorecard that also shows USD- and inflation-adjusted returns. Earn first from business-to-business (B2B) data, reports and widgets sold to asset management companies (AMCs), brokers, digital platforms and media. Add a cheap premium tier, and take referral income only through SECP-licensed partners once a lawyer has confirmed it is allowed.

The opportunity is real. Pakistan's fund industry holds about **Rs 4.72 trillion across 400–520 funds and plans** ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/29/mutual-fund-industry-adds-rs-520-billion-in-one-year/); [PACRA](https://www.pacra.com/view/storage/app/PACRA%20Research%20-%20Mutual%20Funds%20-%20March'26_1772786363.pdf)). Most investors still buy through bank-owned channels. Actual fees on otherwise similar money-market funds range from **0.06% to 1.25%** ([HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.pk/investing)). Pakistan also has no SPIVA-style scorecard comparing active funds with the index. Fund explainers draw roughly **3x the median views** on Pakistan's leading investing YouTube channel.

Three facts cap the upside:

- **Small buyer pool.** There are only about **0.77 million individual fund investors** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/08/17/pakistans-mutual-fund-industry-expands-nearly-sevenfold-in-six-years-reaches-rs3-93-trillion/)).
- **A funded, free rival already exists.** HalalWallet.pk, backed by a Silicon Valley venture studio, gives away fee and active-vs-index comparisons for the Shariah half of the market at no charge.
- **The legal line has moved.** The **January 2026 Research Analyst Regulations amendment** now requires SECP registration and reaches digital and social-media publishers. Its exact scope has not been verified.

My arithmetic puts break-even at about **1,300 paying subscribers, or about 7 B2B clients, or Rs 1.6–3.1 billion of referred assets**. Realistic revenue at maturity is roughly **$100–300k a year** unless the business later becomes a licensed distributor or adviser. Spend three months and well under a million rupees testing demand before building anything substantial. Stop if the kill criteria below are hit.

This is research, not legal or financial advice.

## A Rs 4.7 trillion industry with fewer than a million retail investors

The headline market looks large. Industry assets reached **Rs 4.72tr at end-September 2026, up 12.4% year on year**. Growth has slowed sharply, from about 38% and 68% in the two prior years. Money-market and equity funds shrank in the latest period ([ProPakistani, citing MUFAP](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/29/mutual-fund-industry-adds-rs-520-billion-in-one-year/)).

Not all of that money is in play for a comparison product:

- **Who holds it.** Individuals hold **Rs 2.04tr (43%)**. The rest belongs to corporates, provident funds, banks and other institutions.
- **Where it sits.** Money-market funds hold about **44%** and equity funds about **14%** ([Pakistan Economic Survey FY26](https://www.finance.gov.pk/survey/chapter_26/5_Capital%20Market.pdf)).
- **Who controls it.** Eight mostly bank-linked AMCs, led by Al Meezan, NBP, UBL and MCB, control **about 76% of assets** ([PACRA](https://www.pacra.com/view/storage/app/PACRA%20Research%20-%20Mutual%20Funds%20-%20March'26_1772786363.pdf)).
- **How many retail investors there are.** SECP counted **768,769 individual fund investors** in 2025 ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/08/17/pakistans-mutual-fund-industry-expands-nearly-sevenfold-in-six-years-reaches-rs3-93-trillion/)). That works out to an average of roughly Rs 2.6m each, a figure that large holders almost certainly skew.

Two other pools are growing faster:

- **Stock-market accounts.** PSX unique investor numbers rose **48% in FY26 to 583,052**, and **~85% of new accounts belonged to Gen Z and millennials** ([PSX Annual Report 2026](https://dps.psx.com.pk/download/document/282570.pdf)). They passed **607,000 in July 2026** ([Pakistan Link](https://www.pakistanlink.org/Headlines/Aug26/07/04.htm)).
- **Overseas Pakistanis.** Roshan Digital Accounts have attracted **$14.16bn** in gross inflows. Of that, **$2.1bn sits in Naya Pakistan Certificates and only $153m in equities** ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/10/12/gross-inflows-into-roshan-digital-accounts-reach-dollar1416-billion-by-september-2026)).

The overseas audience wants rupee yield but fears rupee risk. That is exactly the question a USD-adjusted return view answers ("did I beat simply holding dollars?").

The underlying numbers conflict, and the conflicts matter for sizing:

| Figure | Conflict |
|---|---|
| Total assets, Dec 2025 | SECP says **Rs 4,537bn**; MUFAP says **Rs 4,187bn** ([Economic Survey](https://www.finance.gov.pk/survey/chapter_26/5_Capital%20Market.pdf); [PACRA](https://www.pacra.com/view/storage/app/PACRA%20Research%20-%20Mutual%20Funds%20-%20March'26_1772786363.pdf)) |
| Number of funds/plans | SECP counts **409**; MUFAP counts **521** |
| Individual investors | SECP: 768,769. Tribune: about **250,000**, unverified and internally inconsistent ([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2587410/assets-under-mutual-funds-triple-in-three-years)). MUFAP: **375,020** digital open-end accounts in June 2025 ([MUFAP](https://mufap.com.pk/Upload/WebDoc/Communication/MUFAP_Newsletter_-_Jun_202523852.pdf)) |
| Overlap between pools | No data on how many fund holders, PSX account holders and Roshan Digital Account holders are the same people |

Adding the pools without removing overlap gives an upper bound of about 2.3 million *accounts*. That is not 2.3 million people. I use it only as a ceiling.

The long-run case rests on a wide gap between payments and investing. Raast has about **48m users** and digital banking more than **127m** ([INP/SBP](https://inp.net.pk/article-detail/inp-wealthpk/raast-reaches-48-million-users-with-transactions-hitting-rs50-trillion-in-2025); [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/03/03/raast-transactions-hit-rs1-6trn-as-digital-banking-users-cross-127m)). Investment accounts number in the hundreds of thousands. India shows what closing that gap looks like. It has **6.19 crore unique fund investors** ([Angel One/AMFI](https://www.angelone.in/news/mutual-funds/nippon-india-mutual-fund-crosses-4-crore-investor-folios-as-mutual-fund-industry-reaches-27-86-crore-folios)), about 80 times Pakistan's count. Independent comparison brands such as Value Research grew alongside that expansion.

Recent SECP moves are genuine tailwinds:

- The simplified "Sehl" account limit rose from **Rs 200,000 to Rs 1m** ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2643540/pakistan)).
- Investors can now reuse KYC already done at a regulated institution.
- A Digital AMC framework is in place.

Financial literacy remains a brake. Only **26% of adults** have adequate financial knowledge ([IBA/SBP](https://ir.iba.edu.pk/sbsic/2025/program/14/)). I found no sourced Pakistani evidence of fund mis-selling, only opaque bank-branch loads and anecdotes. The "investors are being ripped off" story is therefore plausible but unproven, and primary research should test it.

### Demand clusters at "which fund and how do I start", not at analytics

The strongest demand evidence comes from 868 Sarmaaya YouTube videos with 13.17m lifetime views (my analysis of `/home/user/Files/sarmaaya_videos.csv`):

- **Fund videos outperform.** Videos about mutual funds, ETFs, pensions or SIPs have a **median of about 21,000 views against 6,200 for everything else**, roughly 3.4x. They are only 5% of uploads but earn **15% of all views**.
- **Most fund videos beat the channel.** **76% of them beat the channel median**, against 32% of crypto videos and 22% of forex videos.
- **Top performers are beginner explainers.** "What is a Mutual Fund?" drew **346k views**, "How to open a Mutual Fund Account within 10 minutes" 266k, and "Why ETFs Are Better Than Mutual Funds in Pakistan" 138k.

The finer cut is less flattering to the product's specific hooks:

| Title type | Median views | vs channel median (6,600) |
|---|---|---|
| "Best" or "vs" comparison titles | 9,800 | about 1.5x |
| Inflation, USD or real-return titles | 7,450 | roughly at the median |
| Shariah-labelled titles | 5,750 | below the median |

One real-return video, "Your Profits Might Actually Be Losses", reached 69k. The Shariah result is notable because Shariah funds hold **about 48% of industry assets** ([Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/Islamic-mutual-funds-cross-Rs2tr-59228)).

The lesson is that people search for "where do I start and which fund". Fee, real-return and scorecard analytics are what make the product defensible, but they are weak hooks for attracting users. Shariah status works best as a filter, not as a topic.

These signals carry real caveats:

- The file has no publish dates, so older videos have had longer to accumulate views.
- Topics were labelled by keyword matching on titles.
- Several top fund videos were tied to Mahaana and were probably promoted.
- The small clusters are thin: ETF n=5, pension n=9, Shariah n=8.

Treat this as directional evidence of audience appetite. It says nothing about whether anyone will pay. Advertising will not carry the business either. At sourced Pakistani RPMs of **$0.30–6.40** per thousand views, the channel's entire library has earned an estimated **$4k–84k in AdSense over its lifetime** ([GoTest](https://gotest.com.pk/articles/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1-million-views-in-pakistan); [ytgrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/youtube-earnings/finance/pakistan)).

## HalalWallet already gives away half the idea, so the gap is narrower than it looks

The proposed product has three pillars: fee transparency, real and USD returns, and an active-vs-index scorecard. Each is partly covered somewhere, but nobody covers all three across the whole market.

| Player | What it offers | How it earns | Threat to this idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HalalWallet.pk** (a Niya venture-studio company) | Free comparison of Islamic funds, ETFs, KMI-30 index funds and Naya Pakistan Certificates. Shows actual fee vs cap, TER and returns. Publishes "Halal Transparency" grades and flags AMCs with no verifiable Shariah adviser. Ran an informal FY26 index-vs-active test | Undisclosed. Outbound links carry referral tags, which suggests affiliate income. Says "No provider pays for placement" | **High.** It is the closest analogue: free, funded and already shipping two of the three pillars for the Shariah half of the market |
| **Sarmaaya.pk** | Data portal, Zar portfolio app, training, a large YouTube channel, PSX-authorised data redistribution | Freemium. Fund comparison is locked to the Premium plan ([Sarmaaya](https://sarmaaya.pk/mutual-funds/compare)) | Medium. It has the audience and data licences. Also a likely partner or buyer |
| **Mahaana Wealth** | SECP-licensed digital AMC and investment adviser. Robo portfolios, Islamic VPS, an index ETF | Management fees on about **Rs 3.8bn** of assets (Dec 2025) ([PACRA](https://pacra.com/finalPr/MTU1NTc=)) | Medium. Competes for first-time investors. Also the most natural referral partner |
| **MUFAP** | Free daily NAVs, monthly category benchmarks, peer averages and 365-day rankings of all funds ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40338855/collective-investment-schemes-secp-sets-standardised-performance-benchmarks)) | Industry association | Makes raw data a commodity. It is the data source, not the consumer interface |
| **PACRA (and VIS)** | Peer-relative 1–5 star rankings over 1, 3 and 5 years, issued twice a year as PDFs. Sector study adds downside deviation for 66–81 rated funds ([PACRA methodology](https://pacra.com/view/storage/app/storage/methodologies/06-29-2022-12-32-31amPerformance%20Ranking_AF_Jun22.pdf)) | **Paid for by the AMCs being ranked** | Low as a consumer product. Its issuer-paid model is the credibility gap an investor-side scorecard can exploit |
| AMC apps, brokers (KTrade, Finqalab), SEO sites (FinHisaab, PakFinance, Pakera), etfs.com.pk | Single-house apps, equity trading, "best funds 2026" listicles, ETF data | Commissions, trading fees, ads | Low individually, but they contest the same search terms ([PakFinance](https://pakfinance.pk/guides/best-mutual-funds-pakistan/)) |

HalalWallet deserves an honest look because it already does what the founder planned to differentiate on:

- **Actual fees.** It shows MCB Alhamra charging **0.27% against a 1.25% cap**.
- **The full fee spread.** It puts money-market fees at **0.06% (Atlas) up to the 1.25% cap**.
- **An index-vs-active verdict.** It reports **KMI-30 at +39.18% in FY26 against +33.84% for the best large active Islamic equity fund**, and concludes that "no large active fund beat the index" ([HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.pk/investing)).
- **Funding and talent.** It has a venture studio behind it.

Its visible limits leave the opening:

- It covers Shariah products only. Conventional funds still hold just over half of assets.
- It covers only a subset of providers.
- I found no evidence that it shows USD- or inflation-adjusted returns.
- Its scorecard is a one-off FY26 comparison, not a methodology that removes survivorship bias.
- Nothing reviewed suggests Urdu or video and WhatsApp distribution.

Assume HalalWallet adds conventional funds within 12–18 months. Any plan that depends on it not doing so is fragile.

The defensible gap is therefore specific. It has five parts:

- **Full-market coverage**: conventional, Shariah, VPS, fixed-rate plans and ETFs.
- **Returns after loads and actual TER, by channel.** Bank, digital and direct-to-AMC routes give different results.
- **USD- and CPI-adjusted returns.**
- **A repeatable, published, survivorship-aware scorecard paid for by investors, not issuers.**
- **Urdu-first beginner content** distributed through YouTube and WhatsApp.

The rupee-adjustment pillar is more than a gimmick. My earlier arithmetic put the KSE-100 at **about 14.4% a year in rupees but about 5.1% a year in dollars** from its April 2008 peak to its December 2025 record. In 2022–23 the index was flat in rupees while the rupee slid from about 178 to over 280 per dollar, which halved its dollar value ([Kahloon](https://kahloon.substack.com/p/the-kse-100-crash-you-could-have)). No reviewed Pakistani site shows this routinely. That absence is inferred from the sites reviewed, not proven.

A Pakistani scorecard has an honest complication that should become a feature. The global evidence favours indexing:

- **87.7% of US-domiciled emerging-market funds** trailed their benchmark over 10 years ([SPIVA U.S. YE2025](https://www.ariesadvisors.com/files/spiva-us-year-end-2025.pdf)).
- **About 73% of Indian large-cap funds** trailed over 10 years ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/35917-73-of-indian-large-cap-and-82-of-mid-and-small-cap-funds-have-underperformed-sp-india-benchmarks-over-a-10-year-period)).

Pakistani passive products are not cheap, though. The Meezan Pakistan ETF reported a **TER of about 0.94–0.95%** in September 2026 ([PSX](https://dps.psx.com.pk/download/document/285767.pdf)). One aggregator lists the KSE Meezan Index Fund at **2.28% TER plus a 2% load**, which may be out of date ([IFN Investor](https://ifninvestor.com/fund/kse-meezan-index-fund/)). ETFs hold only **Rs 6.94bn** in total.

A credible scorecard should therefore compare active funds with two things: the gross index, and the cheapest *investable* index product after its costs. Comparing only against a gross index would overstate the case for passive. The universe is small (about **51 equity funds**, 27 conventional and 24 Shariah, at December 2025 per [PACRA](https://www.pacra.com/view/storage/app/PACRA%20Research%20-%20Mutual%20Funds%20-%20March'26_1772786363.pdf)), so the first edition can be built by hand. Benchmarks were only standardised by SECP in December 2024. Long-horizon and survivorship-adjusted results will mean reconstructing old benchmarks and tracking funds that have closed or merged. Nobody has done this work, which makes it both the moat and the main research cost.

## The legal line runs between publishing facts and giving advice

Pakistan has **no stand-alone licence for third-party fund comparison**. The licensed rating regime covers only the two credit rating companies, PACRA and VIS ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/licensing/capital-markets/credit-rating-agencies/)). MUFAP itself publishes monthly 365-day rankings of every fund. Factual, rules-based ranking is therefore normal practice in this market.

Two regimes change the picture.

**Research Analyst Regulations, 2015, as amended in January 2026.** The final amendments:

- "now mandate SECP registration for research analysts";
- broaden what counts as a "research report";
- add compliance rules aimed at individuals on social media doing analyst-like work ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/business/secp-revamps-research-analyst-regulations-to-enhance-investor-confidence-transparency/)).

The date is inferred from an APP dateline of "Thursday, January 8". The notes did not find the SRO number, the full text, registration requirements, or whether a fund-comparison website counts as a "research entity".

**The Investment Adviser licence under the NBFC Regulations, 2008.** Any personalised suitability advice, model portfolios tied to a user's risk profile, or robo-advice almost certainly falls under it. Mahaana's dual AMC and adviser licence is the precedent. The minimum equity requirement could not be verified because SECP's PDF blocked automated access.

Taking orders, or receiving loads or trail commission, makes a business a distributor. That means being a MUFAP-registered distributor or a Digital Distribution Platform under SECP's 2021 guidelines ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/document/guidelines-for-mutual-fund-digital-distribution-platform/)).

The table below is my reading of the notes. **Counsel must confirm every row before launch**, especially the rows marked low confidence.

| Activity | Licence position (my reading) | Confidence | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Publishing NAVs, returns, TER, loads, AUM and category from MUFAP and AMC documents, sorted by one stated metric within SECP categories | Likely no licence needed. Mirrors MUFAP's own rankings | Medium | Use SECP categories and benchmarks, state the return basis, add standard past-performance disclaimers |
| Calculators for after-load, CPI-adjusted and USD-adjusted past returns | Likely no licence needed. No source regulates third-party USD returns | Medium | Label as illustrative. Cite SBP exchange rates and PBS CPI |
| Aggregate scorecard ("x% of equity funds trailed KMI-30 over 3 years") | Probably outside "research report" if it names no securities to buy, but the Jan 2026 amendment broadened scope | **Low** | Get a written legal opinion before publishing |
| Composite fund or AMC "rating" | Grey area. Risks confusion with the credit-rating regime and falls within RA scope | **Low** | Call it a "score" or "ranking by X". Publish the methodology. Never call it a "rating" |
| "Best funds to buy now" picks, YouTube or WhatsApp recommendations, price targets | Likely needs RA registration after Jan 2026 | Medium | Avoid until registered, or publish through a registered partner |
| Personalised recommendations, risk-profiled model portfolios, robo-advice | Investment Adviser licence | High | Only through a licensed partner, or later under your own licence |
| Accepting money or orders, or keeping loads or trail | Registered distributor or Digital Distribution Platform | High | Partner first. Register only once scale justifies it |
| Affiliate or referral fees from AMCs without distributor status | Grey area | **Low** | Ask counsel explicitly |
| ETF prices or any PSX market data, "live or delayed" | **PSX licence required**; fees are not published ([PSX](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/product-and-services/data-services-vending)) | High | Use end-of-day NAVs from MUFAP and AMCs, or request a PSX quote |
| AMC-paid placement or sponsored content | Master Circular advertising rules may apply ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/07/29/secp-issues-updated-master-circular-for-mutual-fund-industry)) | Medium | Never sell placement or rank order. Label sponsorships |
| User data | No data-protection statute has been enacted. PECA 2016 (amended 2025) applies ([Chambers 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/pakistan)) | Medium | Design to GDPR-like standards from day one |

**Data access** is workable but not formalised:

- **MUFAP data.** MUFAP publishes everything free, but states that it is "compiled from data provided by our member institutions" ([MUFAP](https://mufap.com.pk/pdf/yearbook/2018/Yearbook/Glossary&Notes.pdf)). It has no open-data licence and no public API.
- **Scrapers.** Unofficial tools already republish its NAVs ([Apify](https://apify.com/bakos_bence/mufap-mutual-fund-navs)). Scraping is technically easy, but reuse rights are unclear.
- **Fee data.** From FY2025-26, AMCs must disclose TER in detail ([Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/secp-notifies-amendments-to-nbfc-regulations-revises-ter-shariah-framework/)), which feeds the fee pillar.
- **Shariah labels.** Use only the fund's own certificate and Shariah adviser. Do not make your own Shariah determination.

The cheapest risk control is to ask MUFAP for written data terms in month one. AMCs have a commercial reason to want to be listed.

## Break-even needs 1,300 subscribers, 7 B2B clients or Rs 3 billion of referred assets

All figures here are my estimates on stated assumptions. **The base cost is a lean MVP burn of Rs 650,000 a month (about $2,300 at an assumed Rs 280/$), or Rs 7.8m a year.** That covers two full-stack developers at Rs 250k each, half a designer or data analyst, and about $150–200 of hosting and tools. It excludes founder pay and legal or licensing costs.

Salary sources disagree widely. PayScale implies about Rs 0.6–1.2m a year for software engineers in Karachi ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/PK/Job=Software_Engineer/Salary/e0f013d2/Mid-Career-Karachi)). WageIndicator gives Rs 95k–341k a month ([WageIndicator](https://acc.wageindicator.org/en-pk/work-in-pakistan/jobs-and-pay/software-developers)). The Rs 250k assumption sits in the upper-middle of the second range. Adding a modest Rs 250k founder draw raises burn to Rs 900k a month and every threshold below by about 38%.

**Consumer subscription.**

- At Rs 500 a month net, 650,000 ÷ 500 = **1,300 paying users** (1,800 with a founder draw).
- At Rs 299 gross after an assumed 15% app-store cut, net revenue is about Rs 254 and you need **~2,560 payers**.
- That price anchor comes from Spotify Pakistan at Rs 299 ([Brandsynario](https://www.brandsynario.com/entertainment/spotify-pakistan-prices-of-subscription-packages-have-been-revealed/)). Value Research's Indian advisory service costs ₹4,999 a year ([VRO](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/fund-advisor/pricing-policy/)).
- At an assumed 2% free-to-paid conversion, 1,300 payers need **65,000 active free users**, or 130,000 at 1%. That is roughly 5–9% of the combined fund and PSX account base.
- At an assumed 4% monthly churn, about **52 new payers a month** are needed just to stand still. Lifetime value is about Rs 500 ÷ 0.04 = **Rs 12,500**, so acquisition cost must stay near Rs 4,000 or below. That rules out most paid acquisition.

Sarmaaya locking comparison behind a paywall while HalalWallet gives it away free suggests that **comparison alone is hard to charge consumers for**.

**B2B data, reports and widgets.**

- At an assumed Rs 50–150k a month per client, break-even needs **5–13 clients, about 7 at Rs 100k**.
- Buyers are few: about **24 AMCs** (top eight with 76% share), a handful of brokers and digital platforms, bank wealth desks, media, and foreign frontier-market investors who want clean English datasets.
- No price points were found anywhere. Validate them first.
- There is a structural tailwind. Since July 2025, funds may no longer be reimbursed for indirect expenses ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/02/07/secp-to-introduce-new-cost-reforms-in-mutual-funds-from-july-2025)), so marketing and analytics spending now comes from AMC budgets. Globally, Morningstar earns about **72% of segment revenue from licences** ([Morningstar 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1289419/000128941926000013/morn-20251231.htm)).
- The danger is capture. Sell AMCs data and tools, never rank positions.

**Distribution or referral through licensed partners.**

- Management-fee caps are **3% for equity, 1.5% for income, 1.25% for money market and 0.75% for ETFs** ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40357852/amcs-pfms-secp-replaces-ter-capping-regime-with-caps-on-management-fees-wef-july-1)). Sales loads are capped at 3% and are zero for direct-to-AMC investments ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/SECP-caps-sales-load-on-mutual-funds-at-3.pdf)). No public trail-commission rate was found.
- At an assumed blended trail of 0.25–0.5% of assets, Rs 7.8m ÷ 0.0025 = **Rs 3.1bn** and Rs 7.8m ÷ 0.005 = **Rs 1.56bn** of referred assets.
- If users mostly pick money-market funds (actual fee about 0.75% × 20–50% share = 0.15–0.375%), the requirement rises to **Rs 2.1–5.2bn**.
- Mahaana, a full AMC keeping the entire fee, reached about **Rs 3.8bn** roughly three and a half years after its 2022 funding.
- Load-sharing instead needs **Rs 26–87m of new inflows every month** (650,000 ÷ 2.5% and ÷ 0.75%).

**Mahaana's revenue figures conflict.** GetLatka estimates **$2.5m ARR** ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/mahaana.com)). Rs 3,827m of assets at about a 1% fee implies roughly **Rs 38m, or about $137k**. The GetLatka figure would need an implausible ~18% fee. Treat it as unreliable.

**Content and media.** Sarmaaya's whole library implies $4k–84k in lifetime AdSense. Sponsorships such as the Mahaana collaboration exist, but no rates were found. Content works as a channel for cutting acquisition costs, not as a revenue model.

**Licensed digital adviser or AMC.** Mahaana's **$2.1m pre-seed** went largely to regulatory capital ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2022/08/23/ycombinator-backed-mahaana-wealth-announces-2-1mn-pre-seed-raise-to-launch-digital-wealth-management-company-in-pakistan/)). Its last disclosed round was in 2022. Pakistani equity venture funding was only **$36.6m in 2025** ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/pakistan-startup-funding-rises-to-36-6m-in-2025/)). Excluding the stablecoin neobank Fasset, it was **about $6.7m in Q3 2026** ([i2i](https://i2iventures.substack.com/p/pakistan-startup-funding-q3-2026)).

### The ceiling arithmetic

The ceiling check explains the verdict. To earn **$1m a year (Rs 280m)**, a Pakistani comparison business would need one of the following:

| Route to $1m a year | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Subscriptions | **56,000 subscribers at Rs 5,000 a year**, about 7% of all individual fund investors |
| B2B | About 190 clients at Rs 120k a month, more buyers than exist |
| Distribution | **Rs 56bn of referred assets at 0.5% trail**, about 1.2% of the industry and 15 times Mahaana |

India gives a reality check. Kuvera, a respected direct-plan platform in a market about 80 times larger, reported FY25 revenue of only **"₹5.0 Cr+"** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/kuvera/captable/)).

A realistic year-3 mix might look like this:

| Line | Assumption | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| B2B | 10 clients × Rs 120k | Rs 1.2m |
| Subscriptions | 2,000 × Rs 450 net | Rs 0.9m |
| Sponsorships | Allowance | Rs 0.2m |
| **Total** | | **About Rs 2.3m a month (Rs 28m, ~$100k a year)** |

## A scorecard-first launch costs months, not millions

Content is the go-to-market. The demand signal says to lead with beginner questions such as "which money-market fund actually pays you most after fees" and "what did my fund earn in dollars". The scorecard and real-return analytics then serve as proof of rigour and as hooks for the press.

The most capital-efficient channels are:

- **Search pages** for queries that are contested but young ([PakFinance](https://pakfinance.pk/guides/best-mutual-funds-pakistan/); [Pakera](https://pakera.pk/mutual-funds-invest-pakistan/)).
- **Collaborations with existing finance educators** such as Sarmaaya.
- **A WhatsApp channel** for monthly fee and return updates, clearly separated from tips, because SECP repeatedly warns about fee-charging WhatsApp "tip" groups ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Press-Release-SECP-Warns-Public-About-Illegal-Trading-Application-Misusing-Names-of-Licensed-Brokerage-Houses-and-Pictures-of-Celebrities-FinFluencers-and-Br.pdf)).

The legal posture is itself a trust differentiator in a market polluted by fraudulent tip-sellers. Build web first, not an app, and do not buy users.

The cheapest experiments come before building much:

1. **A legal opinion.** Five written questions to a securities firm: Research Analyst registration for a comparison site and for YouTube content; "score" versus "rating"; referral fees without distributor status; MUFAP data reuse; presenting USD returns. Send a parallel query to SECP's innovation office.
2. **A MUFAP data-terms request.**
3. **A hand-built *Pakistan Active vs Index Scorecard FY26*** published as a free web page and PDF. It covers all ~51 equity funds after TER and representative loads, in PKR, USD and real terms, against both the gross index and the cheapest investable index product. Its job is to earn press and collect email and WhatsApp sign-ups.
4. **A "fake door" premium page** priced at Rs 499 a month or Rs 3,999 a year, taking pre-orders through JazzCash, Easypaisa or Raast.
5. **15–20 B2B discovery calls** with AMC product and marketing heads, brokers, bank wealth desks, Sarmaaya, Mahaana and business media. Ask each for a paid pilot or a letter of intent.
6. **Eight to twelve beginner videos or posts** testing which hooks convert to sign-ups.

## Conclusion

The verdict turns on one point: the best version of this idea is a trusted public data layer, not an app. In Pakistan the scarce asset is a neutral, documented record of what funds actually earned after fees, loads, inflation and the rupee. Simple comparison is not scarce, as HalalWallet and MUFAP already show. Whoever publishes that record first and keeps publishing it on schedule earns citations, B2B licences and the option to become a distributor later. Whoever starts with a consumer subscription competes with free.

The scorecard should also be honest enough to report that passive is not automatically better locally. Index products here charge about 1–2%, so the scorecard has to test active funds against what an investor can actually buy, not just the gross index. That nuance is the difference between an evidence brand and a passive-investing advocacy site.

Three things would change this recommendation:

- A legal opinion that the January 2026 amendment covers factual fund comparison. That would turn the plan into a registered-research or partner play.
- B2B pre-sales failing. That would leave content as the only path, and content cannot carry costs.
- Proof in year two that users act on comparisons at scale. That is the only evidence that would justify the licensing capital Mahaana needed.

Until one of those is known, the right size of bet is three months and under a million rupees.
