# PSX and Mutual Fund Investment Standard

**Version 1.0, 4 November 2026.** A personal standard for checking every investment in Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX) shares and Pakistani mutual funds, ETFs and VPS pension funds before money moves.

It is built on two research reports in this repository:
- `reports/Top investors 21st century principles.md`: what the best investors of 2000–2026 did, and which of their principles survive independent evidence.
- `reports/Pakistan fund comparison tool opportunity.md`: Pakistan's fund industry, fees, regulation and data sources.

> This is a decision framework, not financial, tax or legal advice. Tax rates, fee caps and market rules change, often in the June budget. Re-check the items marked **[verify yearly]** before relying on them.

---

## 0. How to use this standard

Every investment passes through four steps, in order. Skipping a step is itself a "no".

| Step | Question | Section | Time needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Portfolio gate | Should I be investing this money at all, and in which asset class? | §1–§2 | Once, then yearly |
| 2. Instrument check | Does this specific fund or stock pass the gates and score high enough? | §3 (funds) or §4 (stocks) | 30 min (fund), 3–6 hours (stock) |
| 3. Decision record | Have I written down why, how much, and what would make me sell? | §7 template | 10 min |
| 4. Review | Is the reason still true? Am I beating the simple alternative? | §6 | Quarterly and yearly |

The standard is deliberately biased towards **low-cost diversified funds as the default** and treats individual stocks as an optional, capped "satellite". That bias comes straight from the evidence:
- 85–95% of US active funds trail their index over 10–20 years, and so do 88–94% of emerging-market funds (SPIVA, year-end 2025).
- About 73–82% of Indian active funds trail over 10 years.
- In Pakistan in FY26, the KMI-30 index returned +39.2%, against +33.8% for the best large active Islamic equity fund ([HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.pk/investing)).

Even the investors with the best records recommended this route for individuals: Buffett in his 10-year index bet, and Swensen in *Unconventional Success*.

## 1. The twelve non-negotiable rules

These rules decide which investments are allowed at all. An investment that breaks one is rejected, however good it looks.

| # | Rule | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Emergency fund first.** Keep 6–12 months of expenses in a money-market fund (conventional or Islamic) or bank deposit before buying any equity. | Prevents forced selling in a crash. Klarman, Buffett and Marks all built their records on never being a forced seller. |
| 2 | **No leverage, ever.** No margin financing (MTS), no futures, no borrowed money, no "leveraged" products. | PSX froze prices at a floor for months in 2008, fell about 15% in one week in March 2020, and fell 9.57% in one day on 2 March 2026. Leverage turns these into permanent losses (Archegos, Miller 2008). |
| 3 | **Core and satellite.** At least 70% of equity money goes into the lowest-cost diversified product available (index fund, ETF, or a broad fund that passes §3). At most 30% goes into individually picked stocks. | Most active managers fail to beat the index after fees, and only about 4% of stocks create all net wealth (Bessembinder). Picking stocks is a low-odds game. |
| 4 | **Position limits.** No single stock above 10% of your liquid investable wealth (5% while you are in your first 3 years of stock picking). No single sector above 25% of your stock satellite. | Concentration is a survivorship story: Pabrai −60% in 2008, Nomad −45% in 2008, Tiger Global −56% in 2022. |
| 5 | **Beat the hurdle, not zero.** Every investment is measured against an after-tax money-market fund and against inflation (CPI), not against "making a profit". | When the policy rate is high, a money-market fund is a strong competitor. A 20% nominal return in a 25% inflation year is a loss. |
| 6 | **Measure in real PKR and in USD.** Record every result after inflation and after rupee depreciation. | KSE-100, April 2008 to December 2025: about 14.4% a year in PKR but only about 5.1% a year in USD. In 2022–23 the index was flat in PKR while its USD value roughly halved. |
| 7 | **Never buy on tips.** No WhatsApp, Telegram or social-media stock tips, "guaranteed return" schemes, or paid signal groups. | The SECP warned the public about fraudulent tip groups and fake apps impersonating licensed brokers ([SECP, Nov 2025](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Press-Release-SECP-Warns-Public-About-Illegal-Trading-Application-Misusing-Names-of-Licensed-Brokerage-Houses-and-Pictures-of-Celebrities-FinFluencers-and-Br.pdf)). |
| 8 | **Never choose a fund because of its last 1–3 years.** | Past top performers rarely stay on top: only 28.9% of top-quartile US large-cap funds stayed there two years later (SPIVA Persistence, 2025). Hot funds attract money just before they cool (ARKK, Fundsmith). |
| 9 | **Write before you buy.** No purchase without a completed decision record (§7) that states the reason and the sell trigger. | Buffett, Smith and Druckenmiller all fixed mistakes faster because they could see when the original thesis broke. |
| 10 | **Invest on a schedule.** Make regular monthly contributions, and rebalance once a year or when an asset class drifts more than 5 percentage points from target. Do not move in and out on market forecasts. | Investors in the US lost about 1.2 points a year to bad timing (Morningstar, *Mind the Gap 2025*). In Pakistan, people who left after the flat 2022–23 period missed the +203% rally of FY24–FY25. |
| 11 | **Be on the Active Taxpayer List (filer).** | Non-filers pay roughly double tax on dividends and capital gains (§5). That gap is larger than most fund managers' excess return. |
| 12 | **Use only regulated channels.** SECP-licensed asset managers, PSX-licensed brokers (TREC holders), and holdings you can see in your own CDC account. | Protects against fraud and broker failure. |

## 2. Portfolio gate: how much goes where

### 2.1 Time horizon sets the ceiling on equity

Default ranges for the share of investable money (after the emergency fund) that may go into equities. They are judgement calls built from the volatility record above, not research-derived optimums.

| When will you need the money? | Equity (stocks and equity funds) | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Within 1 year | 0% | Money-market fund or deposit |
| 1–3 years | 0–20% | Money-market and income funds |
| 3–5 years | 20–50% | Income and money-market funds |
| 5–10 years | 40–70% | Income funds, VPS debt sub-fund, money market |
| 10+ years (retirement, children's education) | 50–80% | As above |

### 2.2 The drawdown test sets your personal ceiling

PSX equities can lose about half their value in dollar terms, and 40% or more in rupee terms. In 2008 the KSE-100 fell from 15,676 to the 9,144 price floor (−42%) before trading was frozen, and in 2022–23 the index's USD value halved.

**Maximum equity share = the largest portfolio loss you could live through without selling ÷ 50%.**

Example: if a 20% fall in your total portfolio would make you sell, your equity ceiling is 20% ÷ 50% = 40%. Use the lower of this number and the §2.1 range.

### 2.3 Interest rates set expectations, not timing

1. Check the current **SBP policy rate** and the latest **CPI inflation** ([sbp.org.pk](https://www.sbp.org.pk), [pbs.gov.pk](https://www.pbs.gov.pk)). **[verify each review]**
2. Real policy rate = policy rate − CPI inflation.
3. When the real rate is high, money-market and income funds are earning real returns, so equities must clear a high hurdle (§4.5). When rates are falling, equities have historically re-rated upwards (FY24–FY25: +203% as the policy rate fell from 22% to 10.5%).
4. **Allowed:** tilting by up to 10 percentage points within your §2.1 band, at the scheduled rebalance only.
5. **Not allowed:** going all-in or all-out on a rate forecast. Macro timing is graded "weak" for individuals in the evidence review, and even Druckenmiller lost $3bn on one 2000 call.

### 2.4 Currency diversification

A rupee-only portfolio carries the full risk of rupee depreciation. For long-term money, consider holding part of it in assets linked to foreign currencies through channels that are legal for you, then hold that share steady at each rebalance.

These channels were not researched here, so confirm current SBP foreign-exchange rules and SECP-approved products before using any of them **[verify]**. On PSX itself, some sectors earn in dollars or price in dollars, such as oil and gas exploration, IT exporters and textile exporters. They offer partial protection, with their own risks.

## 3. Mutual fund, ETF and VPS checklist

### 3.1 Gates: any "No" means reject

| # | Gate | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The asset manager is SECP-licensed and the fund appears on MUFAP's fund list. | [mufap.com.pk](https://mufap.com.pk), [secp.gov.pk](https://www.secp.gov.pk) |
| F2 | The fund's category matches the horizon in §2.1. For example, no equity fund for money needed within 1 year, and no money-market fund for a 15-year goal. | Offering document, Fund Manager Report (FMR) |
| F3 | If you need Shariah compliance: the fund has a Shariah adviser and a current Shariah compliance certificate. | Offering document, FMR, annual report |
| F4 | You can see the fund's actual total expense ratio (TER) and management fee in its latest monthly FMR. If the fund won't show you its costs, don't buy it. | Monthly FMR on the asset manager's website |
| F5 | The fund has existed for at least 3 years with a published record. This doesn't apply to index funds and ETFs, where tracking the index is what you check. | MUFAP performance data, FMR |
| F6 | You are buying through the channel with the lowest front-end load: direct or digital with the asset manager (no load, or the lowest load) rather than through a bank or third party. | Offering document, the asset manager's app |
| F7 | For income or money-market funds: no single private-sector issuer above about 10% of the fund, and no significant holdings below investment grade, unless you knowingly accept credit risk. | FMR credit-quality and top-holdings tables |

### 3.2 Score: 100 points

Score every candidate and also the cheapest index alternative in the same category, so you can compare them.

| Factor | Points | How to score |
|---|---|---|
| **Total cost** | 25 | Annual TER from the FMR, plus any front-end load spread over your expected holding years. Cheapest quarter of the category gets 25; second quarter 18; third 10; most expensive 0. Subtract 5 if you would pay any front-end load. |
| **Net performance vs benchmark and vs the cheapest index option** | 20 | Use 3-year and 5-year returns after all costs. Beat both the benchmark and the index option over both periods: 20. Beat over one period: 10. Lag both: 0. |
| **Consistency** | 10 | In the top half of its category in at least 3 of the last 5 years: 10. In 2 of 5: 5. Otherwise: 0. A PACRA or VIS star ranking can help, but note that fund managers pay for these rankings. |
| **Risk fit** | 20 | For equity funds, check the worst drawdown and volatility against the category. For income and money-market funds, check credit quality, weighted average maturity (lower means less interest-rate risk) and concentration. Score lower if the risk is above category or above your §2.2 tolerance. |
| **Stability** | 10 | Same fund manager for 3+ years: +5. Fund size between about Rs 1bn and a size where the strategy still works: +5. Very small funds risk closure or merger; a fund whose size has jumped after a hot year is a warning sign. |
| **Real and USD return** | 10 | Over 5 years, did the fund beat CPI inflation (5 points) and beat rupee depreciation plus about 2% (5 points)? |
| **Transparency and terms** | 5 | Full monthly holdings disclosure, clear redemption terms, no exit load beyond a short period. |

**Decision:**
- **70 or more:** may buy.
- **55–69:** buy only if no higher-scoring alternative exists in the category, and say so in the decision record.
- **Below 55:** reject.
- **If the cheapest index product scores within 5 points of an active fund, choose the index product.**

### 3.3 Fee rules to remember

**[verify yearly]**
- **Management-fee caps** (SECP, from 1 July 2025; [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40357852/amcs-pfms-secp-replaces-ter-capping-regime-with-caps-on-management-fees-wef-july-1)):

  | Fund type | Cap |
  |---|---|
  | Equity | 3.00% |
  | Income | 1.50% |
  | Money market | 1.25% |
  | ETF / index | 0.75% |
  | VPS equity sub-fund | 2.50% |
  | VPS money-market sub-fund | 1.00% |

- **Actual fees vary widely below the caps.** Money-market funds charge anywhere from about 0.06% to the full 1.25%. Never assume a fund sits at the cap; look it up.
- **Front-end load:** capped at 3%, with none for direct applications to the asset manager ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/SECP-caps-sales-load-on-mutual-funds-at-3.pdf)). Digital and third-party channels are often capped lower, for example up to 1.5% at Meezan.
- **The arithmetic:** a 2% load plus a 2.5% TER costs about 4.5% in year one. A 0.9% ETF has to underperform by that much before the active fund even breaks even.

### 3.4 Which fund type for which job

| Job | First choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund, money needed within 1 year | Money-market fund (conventional or Islamic) | Compare actual fee and yield. Check the credit quality of the holdings. |
| 1–5 year goals | Income or money-market fund | Check maturity and credit risk; income funds can fall when rates rise. |
| Long-term equity core | Lowest-cost PSX index fund or ETF, e.g. one tracking the KSE-100 or KMI-30 | Compare TER and loads. The Meezan Pakistan ETF reported about 0.94% TER in its September 2026 FMR, while some index funds charge 2%+ with loads. |
| Retirement | VPS pension fund | Consider it for the tax credit **[verify current Income Tax Ordinance limits]** and the discipline of locked-in contributions. Choose its allocation using §2.1. |
| Avoid by default | Funds of funds from the same asset manager, "capital protected" or complex structured plans, plans with exit loads | Layered or opaque costs. Use only if they pass §3.2 at 70+. |

## 4. PSX stock checklist

Use this only for the satellite part of the portfolio (Rule 3).

### 4.1 Gates: any "No" means reject

| # | Gate | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Circle of competence.** In three sentences, you can say how the company makes money, what drives its revenue and costs, and what could ruin it. | Annual report, analyst briefing transcripts |
| S2 | **Normal trading status.** The stock is not on the defaulters' segment, not suspended, and not under a trading halt. | PSX Data Portal ([dps.psx.com.pk](https://dps.psx.com.pk)) |
| S3 | **Clean audit.** Unmodified auditor opinion, no auditor resignation in the past 2 years, no going-concern warning. | Annual report |
| S4 | **Governance.** No recent SECP or PSX enforcement against the company or its sponsors for market abuse or accounting issues. No large unexplained related-party transactions. | SECP and PSX notices, annual report notes |
| S5 | **Liquidity.** Your intended position is no more than about 5% of the stock's average daily traded value, so you can exit within a few days. | PSX Data Portal volume history |
| S6 | **Shariah (if required).** The stock is in the current KMI All-Share list, or passes the published screens (below). | PSX index constituents, [KMI methodology](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/themes/psx/uploads/KMI_30-Index-(New-Broucher).pdf) |
| S7 | **Position fits Rule 4.** No more than 10% of liquid wealth (5% in your first 3 years), and the sector stays within 25% of the satellite. | Your portfolio sheet |

**Shariah screens** (published KMI methodology; [PSX brochure](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/themes/psx/uploads/KMI_30-Index-(New-Broucher).pdf); **[verify current version]**):

| Screen | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Interest-bearing debt / total assets | Below 37% |
| Non-compliant investments / total assets | Below 33% |
| Non-compliant income / total revenue | Below 5% |
| Illiquid assets / total assets | Above 25% |
| Market price per share vs net liquid assets per share | Price at least equal to net liquid assets per share |

### 4.2 Score: 100 points

| Factor | Points | What earns full points |
|---|---|---|
| **Business quality** | 20 | Return on equity or capital employed above the cost of equity (roughly the policy rate plus 5–6 points) in most of the last 5–10 years, **including the 2022–23 high-inflation, high-rate years**. Durable advantage: scale, brand, licence, low cost. Pricing power to pass inflation through. |
| **Financial strength** | 15 | Low net debt (net debt/EBITDA under about 2x, or net cash). Interest coverage above 4x. Survives a year at a 20%+ policy rate. |
| **Earnings quality** | 10 | Operating cash flow at least about 80% of net profit over 3–5 years. Receivables not ballooning. |
| **Management and governance** | 15 | Track record of fair capital allocation. Dividends consistent with cash flow. No pledging of sponsor shares. Insider buying rather than selling (director-dealing notices on PSX). Minority shareholders treated fairly in group transactions. |
| **Valuation and margin of safety** | 20 | Expected return (§4.5) clears the hurdle by at least 5 points. The price is at a clear discount to a conservative value estimate: at least 25% for strong-governance large caps, 40%+ for mid/small caps or weaker governance. |
| **Pakistan risk exposure** | 10 | Low or well-understood exposure to circular debt, government price controls and import dependence on a weak rupee. Natural hedges such as USD revenue score higher. See §4.4. |
| **Liquidity and sizing** | 10 | Gate S5 passed with a wide margin; the stock is in the KSE-100 or KMI-30 or has comparable liquidity. |

**Decision:**
- **75 or more:** may buy, within position limits.
- **65–74:** watch list only, and set the price at which the valuation score would rise enough to buy.
- **Below 65:** reject.

The bar is higher than for funds because the evidence says most stock picks lose to the index.

### 4.3 The pre-mortem: required before every stock purchase

Write down three answers in the decision record:
1. "It is two years from now and this investment lost 40%. What most likely happened?"
2. What evidence would show me early that this is happening?
3. Is that evidence something I can actually observe, for example in quarterly results, circular-debt figures or a policy announcement?

### 4.4 Pakistan-specific risks to check for every stock

| Risk | Which companies are most exposed | Question to answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Circular debt** | Power producers (IPPs), oil marketing, oil and gas exploration, gas utilities, refineries | How large are overdue receivables from government entities relative to equity, and is cash really being collected? |
| **Price controls and regulation** | Fertiliser (gas pricing), pharma (drug pricing regulator DRAP), power tariffs, autos (import policy), cement (regional pricing) | Can a single government decision wipe out the margin? |
| **Currency** | Importers of raw materials, companies with foreign-currency debt | What happens to earnings if the rupee falls 20%? |
| **Interest rates** | Banks (benefit from high rates), highly indebted companies (hurt), cement, autos and real-estate demand | How do earnings change if the policy rate moves 5 points either way? |
| **Tax and budget** | Banks and sectors that pay super tax; dividend-heavy stocks | What did the last budget change, and what might the next June budget change? |
| **Political and IMF programme** | Whole market | Am I relying on a specific political or IMF outcome? If yes, reduce the position size. |
| **Market structure** | Small and mid caps | Stocks can be held at the ±10% daily price limit for days. The market can halt, and was frozen at a floor price in 2008. Can I exit when I need to? |

### 4.5 The hurdle-rate test (required)

**Expected annual return ≈ dividend yield + sustainable nominal earnings growth.** Assume valuation multiples stay flat; never count on them rising.

**The stock passes only if expected return − tax ≥ after-tax money-market fund yield + 5 percentage points.**

Illustrative numbers only:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Money-market fund yield | 11% |
| After 25% tax on debt-fund dividends | 8.25% |
| Required for the stock (8.25% + 5) | 13.25% after tax |
| Stock dividend yield | 7% |
| Credible earnings growth | 10% |
| Expected return before tax | 17% |
| After 15% dividend and capital-gains tax | ≈ 14.5% |
| **Result** | **Passes, narrowly** |

If the earnings growth assumption is above the company's 10-year average, use the average instead.

## 5. Tax facts to include in every calculation

Tax year 2026-27. **[verify yearly; confirm with FBR's rate card or a tax adviser]**

| Income | Filer (on Active Taxpayer List) | Non-filer | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dividends from listed company shares | 15% | 30% | [Baco Consultants summary of s.150](https://bacoconsultants.com/blogs/tax-on-dividend-income-pakistan-2026-section-150-rates) |
| Dividends from power producers (IPPs) | 7.5% | 15% | Same |
| Mutual fund dividends, equity portion (individuals) | 15% | 30% | Same |
| Mutual fund dividends, debt portion (individuals) | 25% | 50% | Same |
| REIT dividends | 15% | 30% | Same |
| Capital gains on listed securities | 15% | 30% (reported) | [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/?p=239418), [ICT summary](https://www.ict.edu.pk/blogs/tax-on-psx-share-trading-pakistan-2026-cgt-rules) |

The 2026-27 budget reportedly made "no major changes to the taxation of equities or investment income" ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2007912)), but sources disagree on some details. Dividend tax deducted at source is generally the final tax, but dividends must still be declared in your return.

Implications:
- **Money-market and income funds** pay out mostly debt income, taxed at 25% for filers. Always compare them **after tax** with equity alternatives.
- **Growth/accumulation units versus income units** can be taxed differently. Ask the asset manager which option is more tax-efficient for your bracket.

## 6. After you buy: reviews and selling rules

### 6.1 Review schedule

| Frequency | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Make the scheduled contribution. Do nothing else. |
| **Quarterly** (after results) | For each stock: is the thesis still true? Check the sell triggers in the decision record. For each fund: read the FMR and check for fee increases, manager changes and credit concentration. |
| **Yearly** (pick a fixed month, e.g. July after the budget) | Rebalance to target. Re-run the fund scores. Update tax rates and fee caps. Run the personal scorecard (§6.3). |

### 6.2 Sell triggers

**Sell when any of these happens:**
- The thesis is broken: the reason written in the decision record is no longer true.
- A governance gate (S3 or S4) now fails.
- The position has grown past the Rule 4 limit. Trim it back to the limit.
- For funds: costs rise, the fund's score drops below 55, or a clearly cheaper equivalent appears (score gap of 10+ points).
- The stock now fails the hurdle-rate test by a wide margin because the price has run far ahead of value.

**Not a reason to sell:**
- The price fell while the thesis is intact. Re-check the thesis; if it holds and position limits allow, this may be a reason to add.
- News headlines or a market-wide crash. Your rebalancing rule is how you buy in panics.
- A tip, or someone else selling.

### 6.3 Personal scorecard: test your own stock picking

Once a year, compare the following, each after costs and tax:

| Compare | Against |
|---|---|
| Your stock satellite's total return over 3 years | A KSE-100 or KMI-30 index fund or ETF over the same period, with the same cash flows |
| Your active funds | The cheapest index alternative |
| Everything | After-tax money-market fund, CPI inflation, and the USD/PKR change |

**Rule:**
- If your stock satellite trails the index product after 3 full years, cut the satellite in half and move the money to the core.
- If it trails again after 5 years, stop picking stocks.

This is the personal version of the SPIVA test that most professionals fail.

## 8. Automatic-rejection red flags

If any of these is true, stop. No score can override it.

- "Guaranteed" or "fixed" high returns on anything that is not a government security or bank deposit.
- Payment requested to a personal bank account or a mobile wallet instead of the asset manager's or broker's official account.
- An entity you cannot find on the SECP or PSX licensed lists.
- A stock tip from WhatsApp, Telegram or social media, or a "finfluencer" with a paid group.
- A stock that has risen sharply on no public news, with a small free float.
- An auditor resignation, a qualified audit opinion, or repeatedly delayed financial results.
- Sponsors pledging shares or selling heavily while promoting the stock.
- A fund that will not show its TER, holdings or offering document.
- Any product you cannot explain to a friend in two minutes.
- Pressure to decide today.

## 10. What this standard does not cover, and open items

- **Foreign-currency investment routes** for residents (SBP rules) were not researched.
- **VPS tax-credit limits** and **mutual fund capital-gains rates** by holding period were not confirmed for tax year 2026-27.
- **The PSX price limit** of ±10% per stock is from 2024 notices and an undated explainer; ETFs may differ. Check current PSX risk-management rules.
- **Thresholds are judgement calls**, chosen to be strict rather than optimal: the scores, the 5-point hurdle premium, position limits and fund-size guidance. The evidence supports their direction (low cost, diversification, no leverage, discipline), not the exact numbers.
- **The Shariah screens** come from the published KMI brochure. Al Meezan performs the actual screening, so use the official constituent list where possible.
- **Property, gold, crypto, National Savings and government securities** are outside this standard. They should still be counted in the §2 allocation.
