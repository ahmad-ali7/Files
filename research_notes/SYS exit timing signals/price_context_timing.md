## Q4. Evidence on short-term timing: momentum, reversal, PEAD, disposition and tax-loss effects

### Takeaway
The academic evidence does not support an expected rebound over 1–2 months for a stock that has lost about 35% over 3–12 months. Intermediate-horizon momentum, which includes Pakistan-specific studies, and post-earnings drift after bad news both point weakly toward continued **relative** underperformance. Only the very-short-term (1-month) reversal effect points the other way, and it is small and largely consumed by trading costs. The disposition-effect literature finds that investors' tendency to hold losers hoping to recover is costly on average. All of these are small, average, cross-sectional effects with large noise around them. They do not forecast any single stock's next two months.

### Cited Findings
**Momentum (3–12 months)**
- [P, citation verified] Jegadeesh, N. and Titman, S. (1993), "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency," *Journal of Finance* 48(1), 65–91. — [RePEc/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v48y1993i1p65-91.html)
- [S] One summary reports the best strategy (12-month formation, 3-month holding) earning about **1.31% per month**. — [The Investment Scientist](https://investment-fiduciary.com/2025/03/09/returns-to-buying-winners-and-selling-losers/)
- [S] In years 2–3 after formation, winners underperform losers, i.e. there is partial long-run reversal. — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/recognizing-an-investing-signal-that-defied-wisdom-and-endured/)
- [Not verified this session, from researcher background knowledge] J&T report that strategies with 3–12 month formation and holding periods earned about 1% a month, and that part of the first-year excess return dissipates over the next two years. The abstract was not fetched. **The report writer should cite this only with the RePEc link and as "about 1% a month".**

**Pakistan (PSX) momentum studies**
- [S/academic] Shah & Shah (2018, *Pakistan Development Review* 57(3): 253–282) studied 581 PSX firms over 2004–2014. They found **momentum profits at both short and long horizons, while most contrarian profits were insignificant**. Underreaction was the largest driver and cross-sectional risk the second; lead-lag effects reduce momentum profits. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pid/journl/v57y2018i3p253-282.html); [PDR article](https://thepdr.pk/index.php/pdr/article/view/2785)
- [S/academic] Shah & Shah (2015, *Pakistan Business Review*) studied 609 stocks over 2004–2014 using the J&T method. Buying winners and selling losers earned positive returns, robust to excluding small stocks. — [IBA Business Review](https://ir.iba.edu.pk/businessreview/vol12/iss1/7/)
- [S/academic] Ullah et al. (2019) found a strong momentum effect in the PSX-100. An IBA study covering 2001–2015 found momentum stronger in small stocks. — [IBA thesis repository](https://ir.iba.edu.pk/etd/36); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/335343953.pdf)
- [S/academic, counterpoint] An IBA Business Review paper on volatility scaling states that momentum profits are **low** on the PSX. — [IBA Business Review](https://businessreview.iba.edu.pk/momentum-trading.php)

**Disposition effect**
- [P, verbatim abstract] Odean (1998), "Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?", *Journal of Finance*. Based on 10,000 discount-brokerage accounts: investors "demonstrate a strong preference for realizing winners rather than losers". **Tax-loss effects in a Pakistan context**
- [S] Capital gains on listed securities are taxed at about **15% for filers** (higher for non-filers; some sources give 12.5–15% by holding period). Holdings over 6 years are exempt. **Capital losses can be set off against securities gains and carried forward for up to 3 tax years**, administered through NCCPL for taxpayers on the Active Taxpayers List. Pakistan's **tax year ends 30 June**. — [Bolnews on FBR carry-forward rules](https://www.bolnews.com/latest/2021/06/fbr-allows-listed-firms-to-carry-forward-cgt-losses/amp); [Profit on NCCPL CGT rates](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=207970); [Baco Consultants](https://bacoconsultants.com/blogs/capital-gains-tax-pakistan-2026-property-shares-section-37)

### Inferences
- **Momentum vs reversal for this case.** SYS's formation-period return is about -35% YTD and about -29% over 12 months, with -13.8% over the last two months (Q1). That puts it in the bottom group of a 3–12-month momentum sort. The J&T and PSX studies imply that such losers, on average, keep **underperforming** over the next 3–12 months, by roughly 1% a month in US data. The 1-month reversal effect works at a 1-month horizon only, is concentrated in small, illiquid names, and tends to be wiped out by trading costs (literature background, not verified here). SYS's last-month return (-5.5%) is modest, so a strong 1-month reversal setup is not obvious.
- **The data argues against a "wait for a bounce" premise but does not rule out a bounce.** At about 1% a month, the expected momentum drag over 1–2 months is small next to SYS's daily range of 1–5% and October's -8.4% move. In practice the expected benefit of waiting is close to zero or slightly negative, and the uncertainty is large either way.
- **Industry momentum.** Because the decline is sector-wide (Q2), industry-momentum research (Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999; not verified this session) would also classify PSX IT/tech as a loser industry. That is a mild negative continuation signal on average.
- **PEAD.** If the Q3 print is read as a negative surprise (margin compression, unconsolidated PAT -23%, no dividend), the classic drift literature points to continued weak relative performance for about 60 trading days after 26 Oct, i.e. into late January. Emerging-market evidence on this is mixed, and the observed post-results reaction was muted (about -1%).
- **Disposition effect.** Odean finds that the instinct to hold a loser until it "comes back" is, on average, not rewarded by subsequent returns, and is tax-inefficient. Under Pakistan's rules, a realised loss can be carried forward for three tax years against future securities gains. Because the tax year ends 30 June, there is **no December tax-loss deadline** and no year-end tax-loss-selling pressure or "January bounce" to expect in a Nov–Dec window.
- **The kind of evidence this is.** All of the above are average cross-sectional premia over many stocks. They say nothing reliable about one stock over 1–2 months.

## Q5. Practical execution: circuit breakers, liquidity and staged selling

### Takeaway
SYS is liquid enough that a small investor's order is negligible: about 0.86M shares, or roughly Rs 95m, trade on an average day. Price limits are ±10% a day per stock. The main execution risk is choosing a bad intraday price on a volatile day, as on 2 Nov (105.05 to 115.00). It is not market impact.

### Cited Findings
- [P] The SYS circuit-breaker band on 4 Nov 2026 was 98.52–120.42 against the prior close of 109.47. — [PSX](https://dps.psx.com.pk/company/SYS)
- [E] That band is exactly ±10.0% (109.47 × 0.9 = 98.52; × 1.1 = 120.42), which confirms a **±10% daily per-stock limit**.
- [S] Index-level circuit breaker: on 2 Mar 2026 market-wide trading was halted for one hour after the sharp KSE-100 fall. — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/03/02/panic-selling-or-planned-selling-psx-reopens-after-9-plunge-and-market-halt/). The exact trigger thresholds were not fetched.
- [S] Liquidity:
  - 20-day average volume was 862,636 shares as of 3 Nov 2026 ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/psx/SYS/statistics/)).
  - Daily volume ranged about 0.36M–1.8M shares in October and peaked at about 4.95M around the H1 results on 31 Aug ([StockAnalysis history](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/psx/SYS/history/); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/equities/systems-ltd-historical-data)).

### Inferences
- [E] A retail-sized order, say a few thousand shares, is under 1% of average daily volume, so market impact is negligible. Bid-ask spread and commission, not liquidity, are the relevant frictions.
- Limit orders protect against selling into a temporary intraday spike down, such as the 105.05 print on 2 Nov when the close was 108.40. They carry a risk of not being filled if the price keeps moving away.
- Selling in staged tranches over days or weeks does not raise the expected price. It averages the exit price and so reduces the variance of the outcome (regret about timing). This is standard execution logic, not sourced to a specific study here.
- The ±10% limit means even a severe single-day shock caps the one-day loss, but a halt can delay an exit.
