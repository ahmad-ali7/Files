# Competitors and Analogues: Pakistan Fund Comparison / Investment Analytics Product

Research date: 2 November 2026. Each figure carries its date. "Claimed" means a company or promotional statement. "Estimate" means third-party modelling (GetLatka, Tracxn, PitchBook). "Verified" means a regulator, a rating agency, a filing or primary data. The search budget was limited to about 20 tool calls. Some sources could not be fetched (Business Recorder returned 403), and these are listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Who already serves Pakistani investors with fund data, comparison, ratings, robo-advice or digital distribution, and how do they make money?

### Takeaway
The Pakistani market has several layers. AMCs run their own single-house apps (for example Al Meezan). Mahaana is the one licensed digital AMC and robo-advisor, and it is still small. Brokerage apps (KTrade, Finqalab) are equity-first. Sarmaaya runs a freemium data and portfolio portal that puts fund comparison behind a paywall. One new independent comparison site, HalalWallet.pk (a Niya company), covers only Shariah products and already publishes TER, actual fee, return and governance comparisons as of 2026. Free raw data comes from MUFAP and PACRA. No player found combines multi-AMC, fee-transparent, inflation- and USD-adjusted comparison with execution.

### Inferences
- The incumbent model in Pakistan is AMC-captive distribution. Each AMC app shows only its own funds, which creates a structural opening for a neutral multi-AMC layer. That is the role Coin, Kuvera and Groww played in India.
- Sarmaaya's decision to put fund comparison behind a paywall, and HalalWallet giving similar data away free, suggests that comparison data alone may be hard to charge consumers for. HalalWallet's free fee and TER data lowers the paywall value further.
- Mahaana's scale (an estimated $2.5mn ARR on a few billion rupees of AUM) shows that licensed digital-AMC economics in Pakistan are small. A comparison product that monetizes through distribution or referral would compete with Mahaana's app for the same first-time investors.
- HalalWallet is the closest analogue to the proposed product and has launched recently. It covers Shariah products only (still a large share of the market) and only a subset of providers.

## Q2. Fund ratings and scorecards in Pakistan, and gaps in what investors can see

### Takeaway
PACRA (and VIS, not fetched) run peer-relative 1–5 star performance rankings over 1, 3 and 5 years, plus fund stability ratings. These are paid for by AMCs and published as dispersed PDFs, not as a searchable consumer tool. No SPIVA-style active-versus-index scorecard was found. HalalWallet's FY26 KMI-30 versus active-fund comparison is the closest example, but it is informal.

### Cited Findings
- PACRA's Mutual Fund Performance Ranking (Star Ranking) separates funds by relative actual performance within a category. It is billed as "an initial screening criterion." Funds get 1–5 stars over 1-, 3- and 5-year periods (36 and 60 monthly observations), and only funds operating throughout the period are eligible. The current methodology is dated July 2024. — [PACRA Performance Ranking methodology](https://pacra.com/view/storage/app/storage/methodologies/06-29-2022-12-32-31amPerformance%20Ranking_AF_Jun22.pdf); [PACRA report Aug 2024](https://pacra.com/summary_report/RR__13374_13-Aug-24.pdf)
- Rankings are issued fund by fund as PDF press releases and summary reports, for example NBP Sarmaya Izafa Fund: 4-star (1Y), 3-star (3Y), 4-star (5Y), June 2024. — [PACRA summary report Aug 2024](https://pacra.com/summary_report/RR_1339_13364_13-Aug-24.pdf)
- PACRA published a mutual fund sector study in March 2026. — [PACRA Sector Study Mar 2026](https://www.pacra.com/view/storage/app/PACRA%20Research%20-%20Mutual%20Funds%20-%20March'26_1772786363.pdf)
- Large spreads between the fee cap and the fee actually charged show why fee transparency matters. Money market funds charge 0.06% to 1.25%. Alhamra charges 0.27% actual against a 1.25% cap. Meezan Islamic Fund charges about 3% a year (as cited). — [HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.pk/investing)
- Passive versus active evidence: KMI-30 returned +39.18% in FY26, against +33.84% for the best large active Islamic equity fund. — [HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.pk/investing)

### Inferences
- The gaps no one appears to fill in one place are: (1) returns after loads and actual TER, across all AMCs; (2) real (CPI-adjusted) and USD-adjusted returns, which matter in a high-inflation, depreciating-rupee economy; (3) a consistent, investor-paid (non-issuer-paid) rating; (4) a SPIVA-style active-versus-benchmark scorecard across all categories; (5) Shariah screening and governance flags across the whole market, not just Islamic funds; (6) VPS and ETF comparison. HalalWallet partly covers (1), (4) and (5) for Shariah products only.
- PACRA and VIS ratings are AMC-paid, which creates room for an independent consumer-facing rating, as Value Research did in India.

### Gaps
- VIS fund ranking methodology was not retrieved, and neither was the exact PACRA star cut-off rule.
- The MUFAP fund performance page structure (whether it shows TER and loads) was not fetched in this session.
- No verified evidence on whether any Pakistani site offers USD- or inflation-adjusted fund returns. Absence is inferred from what was reviewed, not proven.

## Q3. Global and emerging-market analogues: business models and how they grew

### Takeaway
Analogues fall into three groups. **Data and ratings** (Morningstar, Value Research) make money from licensing and subscriptions. **Distribution** (Groww, Coin, Kuvera, ET Money, Paytm Money, INDmoney) gives mutual funds away free and makes money from broking, credit, deposits, US stocks or premium tiers. **Advisory** (Scripbox, Value Research Fund Advisor) charges subscriptions. Distribution at scale wins on size (Groww), while data and advisory win on margin and defensibility (Morningstar). In India, the regulatory catalyst for direct, commission-free distribution was the arrival of direct plans and exchange-based direct MF access.

### Cited Findings

- FY2025 revenue was $2,445.5mn (+7.5%, +8.0% organic) and operating income $526.6mn (21.5% margin). Segment revenue: Direct Platform (Data, Direct, Advisor Workstation) $830.6mn; PitchBook $671.8mn; Credit $354.4mn; Wealth $251.4mn; Retirement $137.6mn; Sustainalytics $112.0mn; Indexes $87.7mn. ### Inferences
- Pure comparison sites rarely become large businesses on their own. Value-capture paths are: (a) subscriptions or advisory (Value Research, Scripbox, at roughly Rs 100cr revenue scale in a market about 20x larger than Pakistan); (b) licensing data to institutions (Morningstar, about 72% license-based); (c) becoming the distribution or execution layer and cross-selling (Groww, INDmoney, Thndr).
- In India the growth driver was regulatory: direct plans plus exchange or demat access made zero-commission distribution viable. Pakistan's 2025–26 SECP moves (Digital AMC framework, the Rs 1mn Sehl account limit, reuse of KYC) are a partial analogue. Pakistan has no "direct plan" share class split, so distributors still depend on AMC-paid commissions and loads.
- A Pakistani product could start as a free content and comparison layer (SEO plus WhatsApp distribution), add a paid advisory tier priced in the Value Research style (for example about PKR 5,000–10,000 a year), and offer B2B data licensing to AMCs, banks and media. Distribution would come later, through a SECP-licensed partner or under an investment-adviser or distributor licence.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for the date of the SEBI direct-plan mandate (commonly cited as 1 January 2013) or for the 2017 exchange-platform circular. It needs verification.
- No revenue figures for Value Research, ET Money or Paytm Money (FY25). Kuvera's figure is an aggregator floor only.
- Bibit AUM and users (2025–26) and Risevest funding were not retrieved.
- No Bangladesh analogue was researched.

## Q4. Funding environment for Pakistani fintech, 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Pakistani venture funding is thin and concentrated. Equity-only funding was about $22.5mn in 2024 and $36.6mn in 2025. 2026 is up headline-wise ($133.7mn in 9M) only because of Fasset. Without it, quarterly early-stage funding is in single-digit millions. Wealthtech and investing raises are rare, and Mahaana's last disclosed round is from 2022.

### Cited Findings
- 2025, per i2i: $74.2mn across 11 disclosed deals (+121% from $33.5mn in 2024), of which about $66mn was hybrid or debt. The largest deal was Haball's roughly $52mn, with $47mn from Meezan Bank. — [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40402392); [TechX](https://techx.pk/pakistan-startup-funding-74m-2025-hybrid-financing-i2i-ecosystem-signals/)
- 2025, per Data Darbar (equity only): $36.6mn across 10 rounds, up from $22.5mn in 2024. ### Inferences
- A Pakistan fund-comparison startup should plan for bootstrapping or small angel and pre-seed rounds (low six figures to about $2mn, as with Mahaana's 2022 round). Debt and Sukuk structures and strategic money from banks or AMCs (for example Meezan Bank's Haball deal) are more available than growth equity.
- Strategic investors such as AMCs, banks, IGI-type groups and VEF/YC-type EM fintech funds are the most plausible backers. Strategic money from an AMC could weaken perceived independence.
