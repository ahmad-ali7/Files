# Simons (Renaissance/Medallion), Dalio (Bridgewater), Druckenmiller (Duquesne): 21st-century records and methods (2000 to Nov 2026)

Research date: 2 Nov 2026. About 22 searches and fetches. Most figures come from press reports citing unnamed sources or from secondary aggregators. None of these funds publishes audited returns, so every return figure here is an estimate unless stated otherwise. Gross vs net is flagged wherever a source makes the distinction.

## Q1. Jim Simons / Renaissance Medallion: returns (gross vs net), 2008, capacity, fees, closure; contrast with public funds; replicability

### Takeaway
Medallion's 1988–2018 record is about 66% a year gross and about 39% a year net (simple arithmetic averages, not compound rates). It had no losing year. It was capped at roughly $10bn and was open only to employees, who paid 5% management and 44% performance fees. Renaissance's funds open to outsiders (RIEF, RIDA) earned ordinary returns and lost about 19–23% in 2020, the year Medallion made 76%. The edge depends on very short holding periods, capacity discipline, cheap leverage and the firm's own infrastructure, so outsiders cannot replicate it or even invest in it.

### Cited Findings
**Long-run returns (1988–2018)**
- Bradford Cornell's paper "Medallion Fund: The Ultimate Counterexample?" (2 Dec 2019) takes its return data from Gregory Zuckerman's *The Man Who Solved the Market* (2019). It reports an average annual gross return of about 66% for 1988–2018 and no negative gross year in 31 years. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)
- Summary statistics from Cornell's paper: the average annual gross (net) return is 66.1% (39.2%), with standard deviation 31.7% (20.3%). The gross compound annual growth rate is 63.3%. $100 invested at the start of 1988 would have grown to about $398.7 million gross. — [CXO Advisory summary of Cornell](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=33535); [SSRN paper copy (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/470291821/SSRN-id3504766-pdf)
- Over the same period, $100 in the CRSP value-weighted index would have become about $1,910. Perfect monthly timing between the market and T-bills would have reached about $331,288. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)
- Cornell's conclusion: "To date, there is no adequate rational market explanation for this performance." He also said that even if Medallion is simply better at trading, "the returns are so large, it stretches that explanation to the limit." — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)
- Methodological caveat: a 2024 arXiv note argues that the 66.1% and 39.1% figures are arithmetic means and should not be called compound annual growth rates. It also argues that net returns cannot be compounded the same way as gross returns, and it gives one alternative annualized compounded estimate of about 31.8%. — [arXiv 2405.10917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10917v2)
- Net returns were above 29% in every year from 2010 to 2018. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)

**Specific years**
- 2008: sources conflict. One source cites 98.2% and notes that "Zuckerman reports 82.4 percent." Other sources say about 80% or about 82% net. The S&P 500 lost about 38.5% that year. The most likely reading is that 82.4% is net per Zuckerman's appendix and 98.2% is a different measure (possibly gross or a different data source), but the results do not settle this. — [Quantt Medallion history / search summary](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/medallion-fund-history); [Bonds Are For Losers](https://bondsareforlosers.com/a-hedge-fund-that-returned-80-in-2008)
- 2018: Medallion +76%, Renaissance Institutional Equities Fund (RIEF) +8.5%, Renaissance Institutional Diversified Alpha (RIDA) +3.2%, as reported by the FT. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)
- 2020: Medallion +76%, while the funds open to outsiders fell by double digits. RIEF's reported loss varies by source and cut-off date: −22.62% through 25 Dec (HSBC scoreboard), "almost 20%", or about 19%. RIEF had more than $30bn in assets. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswms7wco7as686o8ikg/portfolio/renaissances-medallion-fund-surged-76-in-2020-but-funds-open-to-outsiders-tanked); [Bloomberg via BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/markets/simons-makes-billions-while-renaissance-investors-fume-at-losses)
- After the poor run, outside clients pulled money from Renaissance's public funds. — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/renaissance-clients-pull-out-after-firm-s-rotten-run-of-results-1.1560482)
- Cornell described the other Renaissance funds' returns as "relatively mundane and in no way comparable to Medallion." — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)

**Why Medallion and RIEF differ**
- An investor quoted by Institutional Investor said RIEF holds positions for 6 to 12 months and hedges with factor-based risk models, while Medallion holds positions for a much shorter time. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswms7wco7as686o8ikg/portfolio/renaissances-medallion-fund-surged-76-in-2020-but-funds-open-to-outsiders-tanked)

**Fees, capacity and closure**
- Fees: a 5% management fee and up to a 44% performance fee. — [WealthManagement.com (Bloomberg excerpt)](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/alternative-investments/jim-simons-built-the-best-hedge-fund-ever); [Validea](https://blog.validea.com/?p=22804)
- Capacity: about $10bn, roughly flat from 2010 to 2018. Assets were reportedly reset to about $10bn at the start of each year, and profits were paid out to investors. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims); [Investment Moats](https://investmentmoats.com/money/strategies-only-work-controlled-capital-size/)
- When the fund closed to outsiders is reported inconsistently: Institutional Investor says it has been closed to new outside capital since 1993, while other sources say outsiders were fully pushed out around 2003–2005. A likely reconciliation is that it stopped taking new outside money in 1993 and returned existing outside capital in the mid-2000s, but this is unverified. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/columns/rekenthaler-report/medallion-fund-is-like-no-other)
- Employees held fee-free Medallion stakes in retirement accounts. After IRS complaints, Renaissance ended this perk. — [Accounting Today](https://accountingtoday.com/articles/renaissance-hedge-fund-ends-no-fee-investment-perk-after-irs-complaints)

**Method**
- Robert Mercer, not Simons, is credited with the remark: "We're right 50.75% of the time… but we're 100% right 50.75% of the time." In other words, a tiny edge is repeated across millions of leveraged trades. The quote is secondhand and its exact wording varies. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims); [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/edge/renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund)
- Elwyn Berlekamp reportedly said: "If you trade a lot, you only need to be right 51 percent of the time." — [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/edge/renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund)
- Zuckerman explains the returns by "ample and cheap leverage," which is made possible by consistent returns and a "crazy-high" Sharpe ratio. Campbell Harvey points to low execution costs, low staff turnover and capacity discipline: "They have been extremely disciplined in not taking on too much money." — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)
- Some argue that Medallion is better compared with proprietary high-frequency trading firms such as Hudson River Trading than with conventional hedge funds. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims)

**Simons's death**
- Jim Simons died on 10 May 2024, with an estimated net worth of about $31.4bn. The date comes from a profile surfaced in search. It is widely reported, but I did not fetch a primary source such as the Simons Foundation obituary in this session. — [Value Research Online](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/201289/three-lives-and-three-deaths/)

### Inferences
- Medallion is the strongest counterexample to market efficiency on record, but its returns are mostly the product of a short-horizon trading business with a capacity cap. They are not a portfolio-investing style that others can copy. The gap between Medallion and RIEF/RIDA inside the same firm shows that "Renaissance-like methods" at investable scale and longer horizons produce ordinary results.
- Gross minus net is about 27 percentage points a year on average. Under 5/44 fees, the manager captured a large share of the value created. Because Medallion is effectively owned by employees, those fees mostly moved money between insiders.
- The data comes from a single source (Zuckerman's reported figures, which have not been independently audited), and only the winning fund attracts attention. Both are survivorship and reporting caveats.

### Gaps
- No audited or official Renaissance return series. All figures trace to Zuckerman's book or press leaks.
- Medallion's 2008 gross vs net figures are not settled (82.4% vs 98.2%).
- Medallion returns for 2021–2025, and its status after Simons's death, are unverified. I found no reliable reporting in this session.
- I did not retrieve direct Simons quotes from his own talks (e.g. the 2015 Numberphile or MIT interviews).

## Q2. Ray Dalio / Bridgewater: Pure Alpha and All Weather, risk parity, key years, AUM, LCH ranking, handover, "Holy Grail"

### Takeaway
Pure Alpha (discretionary-systematic global macro) made about 9.4% in 2008. It suffered its first annual loss in two decades in 2020: Pure Alpha II was down 18.6% in August and about 12.6% for the full year. It made 32% in the first half of 2022 but gave much of that back by year-end, lost 7.6% in 2023, gained about 11% in 2024 and about 33–34% in 2025, its best year ever. It gained 8.1% in the first half of 2026. Annualized returns from 2012 to 2024 were below 3%. Dalio fully exited ownership and the board in July 2025. At end-2025, LCH ranked Bridgewater 3rd all-time in net gains ($79.1bn), behind Citadel and D.E. Shaw. It is no longer #1.

### Cited Findings
**Pure Alpha performance by year**
- 2008: Pure Alpha gained 9.4% while the S&P 500 fell 37%. Many secondary sources say 9.5%, but Institutional Investor gives 9.4%. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1jsw5lw26ncfq/In-Disappointing-Year-Bridgewater-s-Flagship-Fund-Returns-0-5)
- 2019: Pure Alpha II's loss was its first since 2000 (Bloomberg headline of Jan 2020). — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-01-07/ray-dalio-s-pure-alpha-ii-loses-money-for-first-time-since-2000)
- 2020: Pure Alpha II was down 18.6% as of August. The full-year figure is reported as −12.6%, followed by +8% in 2021. The "−18% to −20%" figure often cited is the year-to-date drawdown, not the final result. — [Fortune, Sept 2020](https://fortune.com/2020/09/15/the-losses-continue-to-pile-up-for-hedge-fund-king-ray-dalio/); [TechNews (citing Bloomberg)](https://technews.tw/2022/07/06/bridgewaters-flagship-fund-posts-gains-of-32-percent-through-june/)
- 2020 context: in March 2020, Bridgewater reported mixed results as Covid hit markets. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/03/13/bridgewater-hedge-funds-post-mixed-results-amid-market-turmoil.html)
- 2022: the flagship (18% volatility) Pure Alpha gained about 32% in the first half, driven in part by short bets on European assets. It then fell about 20% in the two months to 30 Nov, cutting its 2022 gain to 7.8% at that point. I did not find a confirmed full-year 2022 figure. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/07/06/how-ray-dalio-flagship-hedge-fund-beat-bear-market-in-first-half-2022/); [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/bridgewater-erases-most-of-its-2022-gains-after-two-month-rout-1.1854089.amp.html); [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/bridgewaters-pure-alpha-surges-32-on-european-short-bets/)
- 2023: Pure Alpha (II) −7.6%. — [Yahoo/Reuters](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/1-bridgewaters-pure-alpha-down-195412435.html); [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/bridgewaters-flagship-macro-fund-down-7-6-in-2023)
- 2024: Pure Alpha (18% volatility) gained just over 11% through 27 Dec (Reuters), or 11.3% (Institutional Investor). — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/bridgewater-extends-strong-run-gains-across-flagship-and-china-funds); [Zawya/Reuters](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/investment/hedge-funds-deliver-double-digit-returns-in-2024-urvutebk)
- 2025: Pure Alpha gained 17% in the first half and 33% for the full year (Reuters). Bloomberg and Fortune report 34% for Pure Alpha II. Either way it was the best year in the fund's history. All Weather gained 20.4%, Asia Total Return 36.9% and China Total Return 34.2%. Bridgewater declined to comment, so these figures are sourced, not official. — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/news/bridgewaters-pure-alpha-outpaces-peers-with-33-2025-gain); [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium); [Hedgeweek H1](https://www.hedgeweek.com/bridgewaters-pure-alpha-makes-17-h1-gain/)
- Long-run: Bloomberg/Fortune say the 2025 gain was a rebound from annualized returns of less than 3% between 2012 and 2024. — [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium)
- 2026: Pure Alpha gained 8.1% in the first half (Reuters, 1 Jul 2026). I found no figure for July to October 2026. — [Crypto Briefing (Reuters)](https://cryptobriefing.com/bridgewater-macro-fund-gains-h1-2026/)

**Assets, handover and LCH ranking**
- AUM: about $92bn at end-2024 and end-2025, down from almost $140bn in early 2023. CEO Nir Bar Dea limited Pure Alpha inflows and handed money back to clients; Pure Alpha was about $72bn in Jan 2024, with a target of about $61bn. — [Wikipedia (citing press)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgewater_Associates); [GuruFocus](https://gurufocus.com/news/2759422/bridgewaters-pure-alpha-fund-achieves-99-return-amid-strategic-reforms); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/bridgewater-extends-strong-run-gains-across-flagship-and-china-funds)
- Handover: Dalio stepped down as co-CEO in 2017 and as chair at end-2021. A client letter dated 21 July 2025 said Bridgewater had bought back the last shares held by Dalio entities, and Dalio left the board (reported 31 July 2025). The Brunei Investment Agency then took a stake of nearly 20%. Dalio said he would remain "a client and mentor." — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-31/bridgewater-founder-ray-dalio-sells-his-remaining-stake-in-firm); [Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ray-dalio-sells-remaining-stake-214411051.html); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/46789004)
- LCH Investments (year-end 2025): Citadel is #1 all-time at about $90.4bn net gains since 1990, D.E. Shaw #2 at $79.9bn, and Bridgewater #3 at $79.1bn. TCI made $18.9bn in 2025, the largest single-year hedge fund profit ever. The top 20 managers made $115.8bn net in 2025 and about $969.8bn net since inception. — [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/top-20-hedge-funds-earn-115b-in-2025/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/chris-hohns-tci-tops-hedge-fund-gains-2025)
- Industry-wide, a related LCH-linked study found that hedge fund investors have lost over half of their gross gains to fees. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/hedge-fund-investors-have-lost-over-half-their-gains-to-fees-finds-study/258986)

**"Holy Grail" and principles**
- Dalio (Bloomberg interview, 2011, and his Principles material): with "fifteen to twenty good, uncorrelated return streams, I could dramatically reduce my risks without reducing my expected returns." Other versions say "fifteen or more" or "10 to 15." — [Macro Ops PDF (transcript compilation)](https://operators.macro-ops.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Ray-Dalio-on-The-Holy-Grail-Portfolio.pdf); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/?p=11679)
- The often-repeated claim that this cuts risk by "about 80%" appears only in secondary summaries. I did not verify it against Dalio's text. — [Summify](https://summify.io/discover/ray-dalio-the-one-rule-that-cuts-investment-risk-by-80-Uprafh/)

### Inferences
- The two strategies work differently. All Weather is a risk-parity beta portfolio that balances risk across growth and inflation regimes. Pure Alpha is leveraged macro alpha built from many uncorrelated bets, as in the Holy Grail idea. The 2025 results show both can work, but Pure Alpha's 2012–2024 record (under 3% a year) shows that diversification across many bets did not prevent a long dry spell.
- Bridgewater's large all-time net gains come mostly from its huge asset base and its strong pre-2012 run. They do not reflect high recent percentage returns.

### Gaps
- No official Bridgewater return series. All yearly figures come from leaks or press reports.
- Full-year 2022 Pure Alpha return not confirmed (it was +7.8% through November).
- I did not fetch primary Bridgewater pages on All Weather or risk parity, or Dalio's books (*Big Debt Crises*, *Changing World Order*). Their content is therefore not cited here.
- The 2000–2007 yearly returns were not retrieved.

## Q3. Stanley Druckenmiller: record, mistakes, 2008, 2010 closure, recent positions, principles and quotes

### Takeaway
Duquesne Capital (1981–2010) is widely reported to have averaged about 30% a year with no losing year. The fund was about $12bn when Druckenmiller closed it to outside capital in August 2010, while it was down about 5% for that year. His big 21st-century mistake was buying about $6bn of tech near the March 2000 peak while running Soros's Quantum fund, and losing about $3bn. Since 2010 he has run a family office (13F: about $4–5bn of US equities). He sold Nvidia in 2024, which he later called a mistake, has been short bonds, and holds an AI- and healthcare-heavy book.

### Cited Findings
**Record and the 2010 closure**
- Duquesne "has never had a losing year in its 30 years and has had an average annual return of 30 percent." He closed in August 2010 by letter to about 100 investors, citing the strain of running a large pool. At that point the fund was "down 5 percent" for 2010 and had about $12bn in assets. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/stanley-druckenmiller-closing-up-shop/30897); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/mutual-funds/2010/08/19/a-legend-liquidates.aspx); [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2010/08/23/2003481107)
- At the Lost Tree Club (18 Jan 2015), Sam Reeves introduced the record as "Thirty years, no losses," noting that $1,000 invested 30 years earlier would have grown to $2.6m before taxes. Druckenmiller said: "I was very proud of the fact that I never had a down year." — [Lost Tree transcript (GitHub OCR)](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6); [Cove Street PDF (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/408239829/Druckenmiller-Speech-2015-pdf)
- Note: the figures are reportedly gross of taxes, and it is not clear whether they are gross or net of fees. I found no confirmation of Duquesne's final 2010 return, so whether the no-losing-year streak survived 2010 is unverified. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/stanley-druckenmiller-closing-up-shop/30897)

**The 2000 tech mistake (Lost Tree speech)**
- He describes buying "$6 billion worth of tech stocks" near the top after first putting in "200 million… in about February." He "had lost $3 billion in that one play," and was "just an emotional basket case and couldn't help myself." On what he learned: "I didn't learn anything. I already knew that I wasn't supposed to do that." — [Lost Tree transcript](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2018/07/stanley-druckenmiller-my-biggest-mistake-and-what-i-learned-from-it)

**2007–2008**
- He saw that "this thing was going to end in a spectacular housing bust," and said "'07 and '08 were – they were a lot of fun." His specific 2008 positions and return were not found. — [Lost Tree transcript](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6)

**Principles (direct quotes, Lost Tree 2015)**
- "The only way to make long-term returns in our business that are superior is by being a pig."
- "Put all your eggs in one basket and then watch the basket very carefully."
- What he learned from Soros: when you see it, "bet big." Soros on the 1992 sterling trade: "We should have 200 percent of our net worth in this trade, not 100 percent."
- "I had a higher batting average; Soros had a much bigger slugging percentage."
- "Earnings don't move the overall market; it's the Federal Reserve Board… It's liquidity that moves markets."
- "Never, ever invest in the present… visualize the situation 18 months from now… If you invest in the present, you're going to get run over."
- On reversing: "you have to change, and that's how I've always managed money"; "a lot of times I'm wrong."
- Source for all of the above: [Lost Tree transcript (OCR, verify against PDF)](https://gist.github.com/timhwang21/e6a2b24e064182dd9099ad00e4f4f9a6); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2019/08/stanley-druckenmiller-never-ever-invest-in-the-present)
- The well-known line "It's not whether you're right or wrong, but how much money you make when you're right and how much you lose when you're wrong" is attributed to Druckenmiller via Soros. I did not find it in a primary transcript in this session. — [Business Insider India quote list](https://www.businessinsider.in/miscellaneous/put-all-your-eggs-in-one-basket-and-watch-the-basket-very-carefully-here-are-13-brilliant-quotes-from-billionaire-investor-stanley-druckenmiller/slidelist/68189821.cms)

**Recent positions (13F and interviews)**
- Nvidia: Duquesne cut Nvidia by 72% in Q1 2024 and then sold the rest (pre-split prices of about $800–950), on valuation grounds. In a Bloomberg TV interview he called selling all of it "a mistake" and said he is not past owning it again. He kept AI exposure through infrastructure names. — [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/billionaire-investor-admits-selling-nvidia-nasdaqnvda-was-a-mistake); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/billionaire-investor-stanley-druckenmiller-says-he-regrets-selling-his-entire-nvidia-stake-and-is-looking-to-buy-again/)

### Inferences
- His 13F is a poor picture of his macro book. It excludes currencies, Treasuries and futures, which is where his main macro calls (short bonds, dollar views, gold and copper) sit. It also shows heavy turnover, which fits his "willingness to reverse" principle (e.g. Nvidia in, out, then regretted).
- The 2000 episode, losing about $3bn by chasing tech after being right early, is the clearest documented instance of him breaking his own rules. It contrasts with Duquesne's record over the same 30 years.
- The roughly 30% a year with no losing year is self- and press-reported. Since 2010 he has managed only family money, so there is no public return record for 2010–2026.

### Cross-cutting replicability and survivorship caveats (applies to all three)
- None of the three managers publishes audited returns, so the well-known figures rest on a book (Zuckerman), press leaks, or self-report. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims); [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium)
- Fees take a large share of hedge fund returns: LCH found investors lost over half their gross gains to fees, and Medallion's gross–net gap is about 27 percentage points a year. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/hedge-fund-investors-have-lost-over-half-their-gains-to-fees-finds-study/258986); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=33535)
- Inference: all three are survivors selected for fame. The more transferable lessons are Dalio's diversification math, and Druckenmiller's focus on liquidity, asymmetric sizing and cutting losses. Medallion's edge cannot be transferred: it depends on capacity limits, short-horizon infrastructure, cheap leverage and proprietary data.
