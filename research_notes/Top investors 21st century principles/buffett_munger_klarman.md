# Buffett, Munger (Berkshire Hathaway) and Seth Klarman (Baupost): 21st-Century Records and Methods (2000 – Nov 2026)

Source conventions used below:
- **[VERIFIED]** means the figure comes from a primary document: a Berkshire shareholder letter (berkshirehathaway.com PDFs, read in full text), a company SEC filing, or a full interview transcript.
- **[PRESS]** means a press or aggregator report.
- **[COMPUTED]** means I calculated it myself from the annual figures in Berkshire's own performance table. These are geometric CAGRs.
- Berkshire's figures are **per-share market value (stock price) changes**. The S&P 500 figures are **total return with dividends**. Neither is "net of fees" in a fund sense, because Berkshire charges no fee. Baupost figures are fund returns and are noted as net or gross wherever a source says which.

## Q1. Berkshire per-share compounding 2000–2025 vs S&P 500 total return, and how outperformance changed from pre-2000

### Takeaway
Berkshire's stock compounded about **10.5%/yr from 2000 to 2025, against 8.1% for the S&P 500 (total return)**, a lead of roughly 2.4 points a year [COMPUTED from the 2025 letter table]. From 1965 to 1999 it compounded about **27.2%, against 12.4%**, a lead of roughly 15 points. All of the post-2000 edge was earned in 2000–2009, when the stock gained about 5.9%/yr while the S&P lost about 1%/yr. From 2010 to 2025 Berkshire slightly **trailed** the index, at about 13.5% vs 14.1%/yr. Buffett predicted this shrinkage repeatedly ("huge sums forge their own anchor").

### Cited Findings
- **Official long-run record (2025 letter, the first written by Greg Abel, dated Feb 28, 2026):** compounded annual gain 1965–2025 of **19.7%** for Berkshire per-share market value vs **10.5%** for the S&P 500 with dividends. Overall gain 1964–2025 was **6,099,294% vs 46,061%** [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf). Morningstar reports 19.8% annualized for the stock, plus 18.1% CAGR in book value per share, over 1965–2025 [PRESS] — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/berkshire-hathaway-greg-abel-goes-conservative-with-his-debut-annual-letter-shareholders). (My own recomputation from the annual rows gives 19.8%. The small gap comes from rounding and the non-calendar 1965–67 periods.)
- **Annual % change, Berkshire market value vs S&P 500 TR, 2000–2025** [VERIFIED, 2025 letter table]: 2000 26.6 vs (9.1); 2001 6.5 vs (11.9); 2002 (3.8) vs (22.1); 2003 15.8 vs 28.7; 2004 4.3 vs 10.9; 2005 0.8 vs 4.9; 2006 24.1 vs 15.8; 2007 28.7 vs 5.5; 2008 (31.8) vs (37.0); 2009 2.7 vs 26.5; 2010 21.4 vs 15.1; 2011 (4.7) vs 2.1; 2012 16.8 vs 16.0; 2013 32.7 vs 32.4; 2014 27.0 vs 13.7; 2015 (12.5) vs 1.4; 2016 23.4 vs 12.0; 2017 21.9 vs 21.8; 2018 2.8 vs (4.4); 2019 11.0 vs 31.5; 2020 2.4 vs 18.4; 2021 29.6 vs 28.7; 2022 4.0 vs (18.1); 2023 15.8 vs 26.3; 2024 25.5 vs 25.0; **2025 10.9 vs 17.9** — [Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)
- **Period CAGRs [COMPUTED from the table above]:**
  - 2000–2025 (26 yrs): BRK **10.51%** vs S&P **8.08%**. $1 grew to 13.4x vs 7.5x. BRK beat the S&P in 16 of 26 years.
  - 2000–2009: BRK **5.86%** vs S&P **−0.95%**.
  - 2010–2019: BRK 13.09% vs S&P 13.57%.
  - 2010–2025: BRK 13.52% vs S&P 14.13%.
  - 2020–2025: BRK 14.25% vs S&P 15.08%.
  - 2016–2025: BRK 14.34% vs S&P 14.82%.
  - Pre-2000 comparison: 1965–1999 BRK **27.19%** vs S&P **12.43%**; 1980–89 BRK 39.1% vs 17.4%; 1990–99 BRK 20.5% vs 18.2%.
  — computed from [Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)
- **Book value per share**, last published in the 2018 letter, which gives annual BV changes through 2018 and a 1965–2018 CAGR of **18.7%** (BV) vs 20.5% (market value) vs 9.7% (S&P) [VERIFIED]. Annual BV changes 2000–2018: 6.5, (6.2), 10.0, 21.0, 10.5, 6.4, 18.4, 11.0, (9.6), 19.8, 13.0, 4.6, 14.4, 18.2, 8.3, 6.4, 10.7, 23.0, 0.4 — [Berkshire 2018 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2018ltr.pdf). [COMPUTED] BV CAGR was **9.49%** for 2000–2018 against 4.87% for the S&P over the same years, and 24.2% for 1965–1999.
- Buffett explained why book value was dropped in the 2018 letter: it is "making its farewell appearance." Reasons he gave were that marketable equities are carried at market while operating businesses are carried far below value ("a mismark that has grown in recent years"), and that buybacks above book raise intrinsic value while lowering book value [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2018 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2018ltr.pdf)
- 2009 letter: "our performance advantage has shrunk dramatically as our size has grown, an unpleasant trend that is certain to continue… huge sums forge their own anchor and our future advantage, if any, will be a small fraction of our historical edge." Also: "our defense has been better than our offense" [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2009 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)
- 2010 letter: "The bountiful years, we want to emphasize, will never return. The huge sums of capital we currently manage eliminate any chance of exceptional performance… we hope to average several points better than the S&P" [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2010 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2010ltr.pdf)
- Abel's 2025 letter: "At Berkshire's scale, the math of compounding works against us – a reality long understood and best acknowledged plainly." 2025 operating earnings were $44.5B, against $47.4B in 2024 [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)

### Inferences
- The post-2000 outperformance came almost entirely from the defensive 2000–2002 and 2008 years. That fits Buffett's own description ("defense better than offense") and the large cash buffer. Since 2010, Berkshire has behaved like a lower-volatility, roughly market-matching compounder rather than a market-beater.
- The stated aim of beating the S&P by "several points" (2010 letter) was not met over 2010–2025 on a market-price basis.

### Gaps
- Berkshire no longer publishes per-share book value or intrinsic value, so there is no official BV series after 2018. Morningstar's 18.1% BV CAGR to 2025 is a press figure I could not reconcile.
- The 2026 year-to-date performance and Q3 2026 results (due Nov 7, 2026) were not available when this was researched.

## Q2. Major 21st-century deals: dates, amounts, rationale, outcomes (wins and mistakes)

### Takeaway
Since 2000 Berkshire's capital has gone mainly into (a) whole-company acquisitions of capital-intensive US "infrastructure" businesses (BNSF, BHE utilities, PCC, Pilot, OxyChem), (b) crisis-era structured deals with high fixed coupons plus warrants (Goldman Sachs, GE, Wrigley, Bank of America, Occidental), and (c) a concentrated equity book dominated by Apple and, after 2019, by the five Japanese trading houses. Clear wins: Apple, BofA, BYD, the Japanese houses, GS/GE. Admitted mistakes: ConocoPhillips (2008), the Irish banks, Energy Future Holdings, IBM, PCC (an ~$11B write-down), and Kraft Heinz (repeated write-downs). In 2023–2025 the portfolio was a net seller and cash reached records, around $382B in Q3 2025 and $397.4B in Q1 2026. Net buying resumed under Abel in Q2 2026.

### Cited Findings
**2008 crisis deals (GS, GE, Wrigley)**
- 2008 letter: "we made purchases totaling $14.5 billion in fixed-income securities issued by Wrigley, Goldman Sachs and General Electric… which carry high current yields… in each… we also acquired a substantial equity participation as a bonus." To fund them Buffett sold parts of J&J, P&G and ConocoPhillips. He also wrote: "I will not trade even a night's sleep for the chance of extra profits" [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)
- 2009 letter: "At the very peak of the crisis, we poured $15.5 billion into a business world that could otherwise look only to the federal government for help. Of that, $9 billion went to bolster capital at three highly-regarded and previously-secure American businesses… The remaining $6.5 billion [funded] Wrigley." (The three were Goldman Sachs at $5B, GE at $3B and Swiss Re; Berkshire had committed $3B to GE.) [VERIFIED for quote] — [Berkshire 2009 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)
- Goldman redeemed Berkshire's $5B preferred (Series G, 10% coupon, issued Oct 2008) for **$5.5B plus accrued dividends on April 18, 2011**. Goldman booked a $1.64B preferred-dividend charge. Berkshire also held a warrant on 43.5M shares at $115 [VERIFIED, Goldman filings and press release] — [Goldman Sachs press release](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2011/redeem-stock); [GS 10-K FY2011](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000886982/000119312512085822/d276319d10k.htm). Press puts the total profit on the preferred at about $3.7B [PRESS] — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/warren-buffett-invested-5b-goldman-195420885.html). Berkshire later sold most of its Goldman common in 2020 [PRESS] — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2020/05/16/business/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway-goldman-sachs/index.html)
- 2011 letter: "Swiss Re, Goldman Sachs and General Electric paid us an aggregate of $12.8 billion to redeem securities that were producing about $1.2 billion of pre-tax earnings for Berkshire" [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf)
- 2008 put derivatives: equity-index put contracts on the S&P 500 and the Nikkei, among others, totaling $37.1B notional with $4.9B of premiums received. The year-end 2008 mark was a $10B liability [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)

**2008 mistakes**

**BYD (2008)**
- Berkshire bought 225M BYD H-shares at a cost of **$232M**. The year-end 2017 market value was $1,961M; year-end 2020 was $5,897M [VERIFIED, holdings tables] — [Berkshire 2017 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2017ltr.pdf); [Berkshire 2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf). Trimming began in 2022. The 10-Q for the period ending June 30, 2025 showed the position at zero, and a Berkshire spokesperson confirmed the full exit in September 2025 [PRESS] — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/21/buffett-munger-byd-exits-stake.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ywang/2025/09/22/warren-buffetts-berkshire-hathaway-sells-entire-stake-in-chinese-ev-giant-byd/). The idea came via Li Lu and Munger (see Q4). Press estimates of the total gain (~4,000%) vary and are unverified.

**Burlington Northern Santa Fe (announced Nov 2009, closed Feb 2010)**
- 2010 letter: "Making this purchase increased our share count by 6% and used $22 billion of cash… owning this railroad will increase Berkshire's 'normal' earning power by nearly 40% pre-tax." Rationale: "railroads have major cost and environmental advantages over trucking" [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2010 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2010ltr.pdf). [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf). (The 2010 cash and shares paid were for the ~77% Berkshire did not already own. The 2008 letter shows Berkshire had already reached a 20% holding in BNSF [VERIFIED] — [2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf).)

The letter included a long passage on why he would welcome a low IBM price during its buybacks [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf)

Insurance float was $176B (vs $88B in 2015) [VERIFIED] — [2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)

### Inferences
- Many of Berkshire's best post-2000 returns came from **liquidity provision on its own terms** in crises (GS, GE, BofA, Occidental): high fixed coupons plus warrants, which is a form of margin of safety. ## Q3. Buffett's 10-year bet (Dec 19, 2007 – Dec 31, 2017): S&P 500 index fund vs Protégé Partners' funds-of-funds

### Takeaway
The Vanguard S&P 500 index fund gained **125.8% (8.5%/yr)**. Protégé's five funds-of-funds gained 2.8%–87.7% in total, with average annual gains of **0.3%–6.5%**. ### Cited Findings

- Final scorecard: final gain for funds-of-funds A–E of 21.7%, 42.3%, 87.7%, 2.8% (D was liquidated in 2017, so its figure covers nine years) and 27.0%, against **125.8%** for the S&P index fund. [VERIFIED] — [2017 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2017ltr.pdf)

Through nine years the index had compounded 7.1%/yr [VERIFIED] — [Berkshire 2016 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2016ltr.pdf)

### Inferences
- Fund-of-funds figures are **net of all fees**, and that is the point of the bet. The index figure is close to gross, since the fee was token.

## Q6. Core stated principles, with direct quotes

### Takeaway
All three investors frame investing as buying businesses or claims on businesses at a discount, using liquidity in crises, admitting and fixing mistakes fast, and avoiding leverage and dependence on others. Buffett and Munger stress quality businesses held "forever," plus incentives and psychology. Klarman stresses margin of safety, holding cash when bargains are absent, opportunistic distressed buying, and a client base that allows patience.

### Cited Findings
- **Buffett: price vs value, and buying in panics.** "Long ago, Ben Graham taught me that 'Price is what you pay; value is what you get.' Whether we're talking about socks or stocks, I like buying quality merchandise when it is marked down." [VERIFIED] — [2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: against holding cash for long.** "Clinging to cash equivalents or long-term government bonds at present yields is almost certainly a terrible policy if continued for long… Approval, though, is not the goal of investing." [VERIFIED] — [2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: financial independence.** "We will never become dependent on the kindness of strangers. Too-big-to-fail is not a fallback position at Berkshire." [VERIFIED] — [2009 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: America.** "Our unwavering conclusion: Never bet against America." [VERIFIED] — [2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: mistakes.** "The cardinal sin is delaying the correction of mistakes or what Charlie Munger called 'thumb-sucking.'" Also: "During the 2019-23 period, I have used the words 'mistake' or 'error' 16 times in my letters" and "Mistakes fade away; winners can forever blossom." [VERIFIED] — [2024 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: marketable vs controlled companies.** "With marketable equities, it is easier to change course when I make a mistake. Berkshire's present size… diminishes this valuable option… Sometimes a year or more is required to establish or divest an investment." [VERIFIED] — [2024 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: fees and the index.** "Performance comes, performance goes. Fees never falter." [VERIFIED] — [2017 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2017ltr.pdf)
- **Buffett: buybacks** (the IBM example). "We should wish for IBM's stock price to languish throughout the five years." [VERIFIED] — [2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf). On Apple's buybacks: "That increase was costless to us" [VERIFIED] — [2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf)
- **Berkshire under Abel (codified 2026):** "Invest in businesses that we thoroughly understand, with durable advantages and long-term economic prospects; Partner with high integrity leaders…; Avoid businesses that undermine the fabric of society…; Act quickly and concentrate our capital in a few high conviction ideas; and Maintain discipline and let compounding unfold." - **Munger, "Psychology of Human Misjudgment"** (Harvard, est. June 1995; Whitney Tilson transcript) [VERIFIED transcript] — [Munger 1995 speech PDF](https://www.rbcpa.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Mungerspeech_june_95.pdf):
  - On incentives: "I think I've been in the top 5% of my age cohort all my life in understanding the power of incentives, and all my life I've underestimated it."
  - On incentive-caused bias: "incentive-cause bias, both in one's own mind and that of ones trusted advisor, where it creates what economists call 'agency costs.'"
  - On lollapalooza effects: "you will almost always find four or five of these things working together… What you should search for in life is the combination."
  - He credits the algebraist Jacobi for "Invert, always invert" (in the speech and Poor Charlie's Almanack) — [secondary: Rational Walk](https://rationalwalk.com/mungers-psychology-of-human-misjudgment/)

"I think you need to be in the top 5% to have a reasonable chance" of beating the market. "I don't have the faintest idea what's going to happen short term." [VERIFIED transcript, third-party] — [Steady Compounding](https://steadycompounding.com/transcript/djco23/)
- **Klarman: margin of safety and humility.** "we always need to temper it with the humility of knowing we could be wrong… you must remember to sell fully priced securities so that you are underexposed when things go badly." [VERIFIED] — [FAJ 2010](http://www.safalniveshak.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Seth-Klarman-Interview-Financial-Analyst-Journal.pdf)

### Inferences
- There is a common thread with an important difference. Buffett and Munger after 2000 drifted toward quality at fair prices and permanent ownership, which suits their scale and tax position. Klarman stayed closer to Graham-style bargains, with distressed and complex situations and cash as a residual. That posture paid off heavily in 2008–09 but lagged badly in the 2010s bull market. Over 2010–2025, Berkshire's dependence on cash and defense likewise left it roughly matching, not beating, the S&P.

- I did not retrieve Buffett's Oct 2008 NYT op-ed ("Buy American. I Am."), so it is not quoted here.
