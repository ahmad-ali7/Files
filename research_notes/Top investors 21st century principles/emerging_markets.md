## Q1. Rakesh Jhunjhunwala (India): what he bought, when, why, how much, and his returns

### Takeaway
Jhunjhunwala's fortune came mostly from a long hold of Titan, first bought around 2002 at roughly Rs 3 per share (split-adjusted), plus late pre-IPO stakes in Star Health and a founding stake in Akasa Air. Forbes put his net worth at about US$5.8bn (about Rs 46,000 crore) when he died on 14 Aug 2022. Stake sizes are verifiable from exchange shareholding disclosures. The early purchase prices and the "Rs 5,000 starting capital" story come from his own account and press retellings.

### Cited Findings
- **Death and net worth:** He died on 14 Aug 2022, aged 62. Most outlets cite Forbes at about **$5.8bn (about Rs 46,000 crore)**. One WION report cites Forbes at about **$5.5bn**, an outlier and a conflict. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2022/08/14/indias-warren-buffet-started-trading-with-rs-5000-has-net-worth-over-dollar58-billion.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/ace-stock-market-investor-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-passes-away-at-62-122081400113_1.html); [WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/indian-stock-market-mogul-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-dies-at-62-506649)
- **Starting capital (self-reported story):** He began trading with about Rs 5,000 in 1985. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2022/08/14/indias-warren-buffet-started-trading-with-rs-5000-has-net-worth-over-dollar58-billion.html); [Republic World](https://www.republicworld.com/business/how-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-turned-5000-into-a-50000-crore-legacy-as-indias-big-bull)
- **Titan entry:** Secondary accounts put the first purchase in **2002–2003 at roughly Rs 3 per share (split-adjusted)**, when the stock was an out-of-favour underperformer. I found no primary trade record of the entry. — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/the-big-bull-of-the-indian-stock-market-rakesh-jhunjhunwalas-legacy); [Fortune India](https://fortuneindia-web.quintype.io/investing/rakesh-jhunjhunwalas-titanic-journey/India's richest)
- **Titan stake at death (VERIFIED via exchange shareholding data, Apr–Jun 2022 quarter):** Rakesh and Rekha Jhunjhunwala held **4,48,50,970 shares, a 5.05% stake**, worth more than **Rs 11,000 crore**, about one-third of his disclosed portfolio. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/podcast/current-affairs/how-did-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-make-his-billions-122081600056_1.html); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/titan-tata-motors-rallis-india-among-top-stocks-in-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-s-portfolio-news-216371)
- **Titan stake history (conflicting secondary figures):** Reported as 10.24% in 2012, 8.46% in March 2018 and about 4.5–5.05% in 2022. These appear to be different dates, not contradictions, but the 10.24% figure is unverified. Wikipedia's Rs 7,294.8 crore valuation reflects a different date. — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/the-big-bull-of-the-indian-stock-market-rakesh-jhunjhunwalas-legacy); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Rakesh_Jhunjhunwala)
- **Star Health:** He bought between March 2019 and November 2021, before the IPO. His stake is reported at **about 17.26–17.51%**, depending on source and date. — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/the-big-bull-of-the-indian-stock-market-rakesh-jhunjhunwalas-legacy); [Swastika](https://www.swastika.co.in/blog/analysis-of-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-portfolio)
- **Akasa Air:** He held about **40%**. Wikipedia says this rose to **about 46%**, making him the largest shareholder. The first commercial flight was on **7 Aug 2022**; one WION piece says 8 Aug, Mumbai to Ahmedabad, which is a minor conflict. His last public appearance was at the inaugural flight, about a week before he died. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/business/2022/08/20/rakesh-jhunjhunwala-was-always-bullish-on-india.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Rakesh_Jhunjhunwala); [WION](https://www.wionews.com/business-economy/flashback-friday-revisiting-the-legacy-of-indian-investor-rakesh-jhunjhunwala-626669/amp)
- **Vehicle:** He invested in his own name and through his firm **Rare Enterprises** (the name combines "Ra" for Rakesh and "Re" for Rekha). — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/business/2022/08/20/rakesh-jhunjhunwala-was-always-bullish-on-india.html)
- **Principle ("always bullish on India"):** He held a long-run structural bull thesis on India's consumption and growth and expressed it through concentrated, long holds of consumer franchises such as Titan. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/business/2022/08/20/rakesh-jhunjhunwala-was-always-bullish-on-india.html); [Equitymaster](https://www.equitymaster.com/timeless-reading/rakesh-jhunjhunwala-portfolio)

### Inferences
- If the roughly Rs 3 entry is right, Titan's 2022 price of about Rs 2,400–2,500 implies a **multiple of several hundred times over about 20 years**. This is my arithmetic on secondary inputs and should be treated as approximate. The point is that **one concentrated compounder produced most of his wealth**, a classic power-law outcome.
- His record was built as a **trader and investor** using personal and leveraged capital. There is no audited fund track record, so the "returns" are wealth outcomes, not a GIPS-style performance series. They also carry survivorship bias: we hear about the Big Bull, not about the many concentrated Indian investors who failed.
- His lesson for Pakistan: concentrated bets on a domestic consumption story worked in India because India delivered decades of nominal GDP growth with a relatively stable currency. That macro tailwind is the variable Pakistan has lacked (see Q4).

### Gaps
- No primary (BSE/NSE) record of the 2002–03 Titan purchase price or size was found. The price comes from press retellings.
- No audited annual return series exists. Forbes net-worth snapshots by year (2000s to 2022) were not retrieved.
- Direct quotes from Jhunjhunwala (for example on "buy right, sit tight", or on trading versus investing) were not verified from primary interviews in this session.

## Q2. Mohnish Pabrai (Pabrai Investment Funds): cloning, Dhandho, track record, drawdowns, recent bets

### Takeaway
Pabrai's record is high-volatility and concentrated. By the fund's own January 2009 letter, the funds fell **about 60% in 2008**, against a 30–40% fall in the indices. PIF2 then rose **122.5% in 2009**. Long-run figures are self-reported and inconsistent: Forbes in 2010 cited **35.3% CAGR after fees since 1999**, while a 2025 secondary source cites **12.2% annualized for PIF2 versus 6% for the S&P 500**. From around 2019 he shifted most capital to **India, Turkey and South Korea**, and by 2024 his US 13F was dominated by **metallurgical coal** (AMR, HCC).

### Cited Findings
- **2008 drawdown (PRIMARY, fund letter, Jan 2009):** The funds were "off by about 60% in 2008", far worse than the three indices, which fell 30–40%. In the same letter, a $100,000 investment made in July 1999 and rolled over was worth **$266,300 at end-2008, about 10.9% annualized**. This is a single-investor illustration, not the composite. — [Pabrai Funds letter, 1 Jan 2009 (PDF)](https://snowballing-co.s3.amazonaws.com/media/l_010109.pdf)
- **2009 rebound (secondary, consistent with letters):** PIF2 +122.5%, PIF3 +125%, PIF4 +118.8%. — [Baidu wiki summary](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Mohnish%20Pabrai/3321124) (low-reliability aggregator; figures match commonly cited letter data)
- **Long-run claim (2010):** Forbes wrote that since founding the funds in 1999 he had delivered a **35.3% compound annual return after fees**. This is self-reported. It conflicts with the 10.9% single-investor figure in the letter, probably because of a different start or end date and a different fund (PIF2 versus others). — [Forbes, Apr 2010](https://www.forbes.com/2010/04/10/pabrai-dollar-cost-average-intelligent-investing-entrepreneur.html)
- **Later long-run figure (secondary, undated):** PIF2 at **12.2% annualized after fees versus 6% for the S&P 500, 763% cumulative**. The article is from 2025, but the measurement date is unclear. The same source describes a fiscal-year loss of **-19.2%** for PIF2 that lagged the S&P. — [Acquirer's Multiple, Apr 2025](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/04/mohnish-pabrai-still-finding-undervalued-opportunities-today)
- **Geographic shift (self-stated, 2019):** He said most of his funds had moved to **India, Turkey and South Korea**, where he found better opportunities. Commentators call the Turkey results "a mixed bag". — [GuruFocus discussion](https://www.gurufocus.com/discussion/topic/1746); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/04/mohnish-pabrai-still-finding-undervalued-opportunities-today)
- **India drag:** His Indian investments held back performance in a 2020s Q2 letter. — [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/investor-letters/mohnish-pabrais-indian-investments-hold-back-portfolio-in-q2-scoop/)
- **Coal concentration (VERIFIED via 13F, Q4 2024):** The US 13F book was about **38.5% Alpha Metallurgical Resources (AMR)** and **36.5% Warrior Met Coal (HCC)**, with later increases in HCC and CONSOL Energy. 13F filings cover US-listed holdings only. — [GuruFocus 13F](https://www.gurufocus.com/guru/mohnish+pabrai/current-portfolio/portfolio); [Kalkine](https://kalkine.com.au/news/stocks/mohnish-pabrai-amplifies-stake-in-consol-energy-by-over-347-marking-a-significant-portfolio-impact)
- **2025 drawdown (self-stated):** On a 2025 semi-annual call he admitted to a "significant drawdown" in recent weeks. No figure was given in the source. — [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/04/mohnish-pabrai-still-finding-undervalued-opportunities-today)
- **Tracking-portfolio drawdown (NOT fund data):** PortfoliosLab shows a **56.9% maximum drawdown** for a replica of his 13F holdings since 2021. This is not the fund's record. — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/portfolio/mohnish-pabrai-portfolio)
- **Fund disclosures are private:** Performance data on pabraifunds.com is behind an investor login. — [Pabrai Funds](https://www.pabraifunds.com/)

### Inferences
- The track record shows the cost of **Dhandho-style concentration** ("heads I win, tails I don't lose much" in theory, but deep mark-to-market losses in practice). Investors had to sit through about -60% in 2008 to earn +120% in 2009. Pakistan-style volatility (Q4) would test a local investor's behaviour the same way.
- His long-run CAGR has decayed sharply depending on the measurement window: 35% claimed in 2010, about 12% in later reporting. Treat any single "since-inception" number as window-dependent and self-reported.
- His move to Turkey, Korea and India is a direct precedent for a US value investor going into frontier and emerging markets. By his own and commentators' accounts, the results were mixed, which shows that cheap valuations in EMs do not guarantee returns.

### Gaps
- **2020 COVID drawdown figure:** not found in any accessible source.
- **Coal India:** I could not verify a Coal India position or its timing in this session (Indian holdings are not in the 13F). Do not state it without a source.
- No audited GIPS composite or current since-inception figure is available publicly (the site is login-only).
- "The Dhandho Investor" (Wiley, 2007) quotes and the "cloning" philosophy were not retrieved from primary text in this session. The report writer should cite the book directly or treat these as well-known but unverified here.

## Q3. Li Lu (Himalaya Capital): BYD, Munger, returns, and his book

### Takeaway
Li Lu's Himalaya Capital bought **BYD around its 2002 Hong Kong IPO (under HK$3 per share by one account)**, and Li later introduced the company to Munger and Berkshire. Munger gave him **about $88m in 2004**, which Munger said grew **four to five times**. The famous "~30% annualized" figure is **not audited**. The most specific dated figure is **26.4% annualized from 1998 to about 2010 versus 2.25% for the S&P 500**. One source says returns fell to **about 20% over the two decades to 2017**.

### Cited Findings
- **BYD:** Himalaya invested in 2002, shortly after BYD's IPO. One account says the price was under HK$3. — [Himalaya Capital (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital); [Yearly Investor](https://yearlyinvestor.substack.com/p/the-great-investors-li-lu-the-worlds)
- **Munger allocation:** About **$88m in 2004**, from the Munger family. Munger later said it had grown **4–5x**, and another estimate puts it near **$400m**. — [FinChat](https://finchat.io/blog/li-lu-the-chinese-warren-buffett); [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/investment-strategy/li-lu-the-man-who-impressed-charlie-munger); [Burzovní svět](https://burzovnisvet.cz/2023/09/charlie-munger-investoval-do-spolecnosti-li-lu-temer-90-milionu-dolaru-ze-sveho-rodinneho-majetku)
- **Return claims (conflicting):**
  - ~30% CAGR since 1998 (secondary, unaudited). — [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/222962/how-li-lu-the-chinese-warren-buffett-spots-market-gold); [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/investment-strategy/li-lu-the-man-who-impressed-charlie-munger)
  - **26.4% annualized since 1998 versus 2.25% for the S&P 500**, from a 2010 report; the most specific dated figure. — [Wikipedia: Himalaya Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital)
  - Above 30% in the first decade, about **20% over two decades to 2017**. — [FinChat newsletter](https://newsletter.finchat.io/p/the-warren-buffett-of-china)
  - Called "a rumor" because the firm is secretive and publishes no audited returns. — [Himalaya Capital (wiki mirror)](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Himalaya%20Capital)
- **Book:** *Civilization, Modernization, Value Investing and China* (文明、现代化、价值投资与中国), published **in Chinese in 2020**. More than half covers China's modernization; the rest covers value investing and collected speeches, including the 2019 Global Investor Conference. A central claim is that modernization is itself "a process of compound interest". No English edition was found. — [Yearly Investor](https://yearlyinvestor.substack.com/p/li-lu-on-investing-on-value-investing); [Strefa Inwestorów](https://strefainwestorow.pl/artykuly/analizy/20210422/inwestowanie-w-wartosc-chiny); [Wikipedia: Li Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Lu)
- **No Munger foreword:** I found no evidence that Munger wrote a foreword to the 2020 book. The link runs the other way: Li Lu translated *Poor Charlie's Almanack* into Chinese and wrote its foreword (2010). — [Wikipedia: Li Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Lu); [Himalaya Capital publications](https://himcap.com/publications)
- **Stated principles** (Peking University lecture "Global Value Investing in Our Era", Dec 2024, summarising Graham, Buffett and Munger):
  1. "A stock is not just a tradable piece of paper; it represents part ownership of a company"
  2. "Mr. Market is here to serve value investors, not guide"
  3. "Investments must have sufficient margin of safety"
  4. "Investors should have a clear circle of competence"
  5. "Fish where the fish are"
  — [Marcellus (republishing Himalaya text)](https://marcellus.in/story/long-read-global-value-investing-in-our-era/)
- **On China and EM capital markets:** Li Lu argues that China's state-owned banking system allocates capital inefficiently, that modern capital markets are needed, and that Hong Kong is the only part of China with a fully modern financial market. He also notes that personal consumption is about 40% of China's GDP versus about 60% in India. — [Marcellus](https://marcellus.in/story/long-read-global-value-investing-in-our-era/)
- **Recent view:** He continues to call Chinese stocks undervalued. — [SCMP](https://scmp.com/business/money/markets-investing/article/3370602/chinese-stocks-undervalued-opportunity-says-charlie-munger-disciple)

### Inferences
- "Fish where the fish are" plus "circle of competence" is the most transferable principle for a Pakistani investor. It argues for owning **domestic businesses one actually understands**. But Li Lu's own caveat about immature capital markets (state-directed credit, governance) applies to Pakistan even more than to China.
- His record shows that the **biggest EM wins came from a few founder-led franchise compounders** (BYD) bought cheap at the IPO stage. Without audited data, the size of his alpha cannot be verified.

### Gaps
- Berkshire's 2008 BYD purchase terms (commonly reported as about 225m shares for about US$230m) and Berkshire's 2022–2024 sell-down were **not verified in this session**. They need a Reuters or Berkshire source before inclusion.
- No audited Himalaya return series exists. AUM figures and 13F concentration (Alphabet, Berkshire, BAC, etc.) were not retrieved.

## Q4. Applying the principles in Pakistan (PSX): returns, risks, funds, SPIVA evidence

### Takeaway
The KSE-100 has produced **high nominal PKR returns but much weaker USD and real returns**, because of chronic rupee depreciation and inflation, including a record **38.0% CPI in May 2023**. On my arithmetic, from the April 2008 peak (15,676 at about Rs 62/$) to the December 2025 record (170,741), the index compounded at **about 14% a year in PKR but only about 5% a year in USD (price basis)**. The market has had extreme boom–bust cycles: a 2008 price floor and freeze, MSCI EM upgrade in 2017 then downgrade to Frontier in 2021, and a roughly 200% two-year rally in FY24–FY25 followed by a -14.6% USD quarter in early 2026. Low-cost index products are scarce and fees are high (roughly 0.9–2.3% TER). SPIVA covers India, not Pakistan, and shows most Indian active funds underperform over 10 years.

### Cited Findings

**Boom–bust history**
- **2008 peak and floor:** The KSE-100 peaked at **15,676 on 18 Apr 2008**. Market cap was Rs 4.79tn (**$77.25bn at Rs 62/$**). — [Wikipedia: KSE 100 Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSE_100_Index); [The Nation, Aug 2008](https://www.nation.com.pk/08-Aug-2008/crises-wipe-out-rs176-trillion-from-kse)
- On **27 Aug 2008** the exchange put a **floor** on share prices at that day's close (**index 9,144.93**), freezing the market. The index was still "glued" around 9,187 on 25 Nov 2008. The SECP reportedly advised removing the floor, and Dawn noted the freeze killed volumes while possibly preventing a deeper crash. — [Dawn: Putting floor under the fall](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/318671); [Dawn: Is KSE index freeze beneficial?](https://www.dawn.com/news/324985); [Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/business/index-drops-4pc-as-investors-sell-blue-chips?amp=1); [Dawn: End to stock stalemate not in sight](https://www.dawn.com/news/331677)
- **MSCI cycle:** Pakistan was upgraded from Frontier to **Emerging Markets effective June 2017** (the announcement came in June 2016). MSCI then reclassified it **back to Frontier in one step at the November 2021 review**, citing the shrinkage in market cap and liquidity since 2017. Only four securities qualified for the FM index, with about 1.9% estimated weight. Pakistan became eligible for the MSCI FM 100 in May 2022. Inclusion of China A-shares in EM also crowded Pakistan out. — [Profit/Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2021/09/08/msci-downgrades-pakistan-to-frontier-markets); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40118765); [ETFGI](https://etfgi.com/node/23276); [Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/2022666); [Pak Observer](https://pakobserver.net/msci-strips-pakistan-of-emerging-market-status-after-4-years/)
- **2020 COVID crash:** The KSE-100 fell **about 15% in one week** in March 2020, its biggest weekly loss since 2008. — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2181227/2-weekly-review-kse-100-dives-15-records-biggest-weekly-losts-since-2008-business/)
- **2022–23 crisis (commentary):** The index was nominally flat while the **rupee went from about 178 to over 280 per $** and inflation went from about 12% to 38%. The index lost more than half its value in USD and real terms. — [Kahloon Substack (commentary)](https://kahloon.substack.com/p/the-kse-100-crash-you-could-have)
- **Inflation peak (VERIFIED, PBS):** CPI hit **38.0% YoY in May 2023**, the highest since records began in July 1965. Urban food inflation was 48.1% and rural 52.4%. — [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40245453); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2023/06/01/higher-food-prices-push-annual-inflation-to-record-high-of-38-in-may/)
- **2023–2025 rally:** Over **FY24 and FY25 combined the KSE-100 gained 203% in PKR and 206% in USD** (Topline). In **FY25 alone it rose 58.6% in PKR and 55.5% in USD** (one source says 57.1% in USD, a minor conflict). The rupee fell only 1.9% in FY25 after rising 2.7% in FY24. — [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40370061/kse-100-beats-us-india-amp-germany-to-emerge-among-top-global-performers-in-fy25); [Geo](https://www.geo.tv/latest/618946); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2606455); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1881738)
- **Rate-cut driver:** The SBP cut the policy rate from a 22% peak in steps (17.5% to 15% in Nov 2024, the fourth straight cut), and later to **10.5% in Dec 2025**. On that day the KSE-100 hit a record **170,741**. Inflation had fallen to **7.2% in Oct 2024**. Later reports had the index above 179,000. — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2507472/entertainment); [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/12/15/kse-100-hits-record-170741-as-sbp-rate-cut-lifts-sentiment); [Aaj](https://english.aaj.tv/news/amp/330450324)
- **CY2025:** About **48–51%** return (sources differ). PSX was ranked the second-best frontier market. — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/31/psx-says-goodbye-to-2025-by-achieving-2nd-best-frontier-market-status/); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2584558/psx-beats-property-dollar)
- **2026 reversal:** The index returned **-14.6% in USD terms in 3QFY26 (Jan–Mar 2026)**. On **2 March 2026** it fell **16,089 points (-9.57%) to 151,973** in a single day, a 9% "worst rout since 2008" with a brief trading halt. — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2553733/psx-tops-asian-markets-with-60-return); [Wikipedia: KSE 100 Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSE_100_Index); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/pakistans-kse-100-freefalls-9-in-worst-rout-since-2008-trading-resumes-after-brief-halt)

### Inferences
- **USD versus PKR compounding (my arithmetic, price basis, approximate):**
  - From the 18 Apr 2008 peak (15,676 at about Rs 62/$, roughly **$253 index-dollars**) to the 15 Dec 2025 record (170,741 at about Rs 280–281/$, roughly **$608**), the KSE-100 rose about **10.9x in PKR, about 14.4% a year over 17.7 years**, but only **about 2.4x in USD, about 5% a year**. This is below the long-run S&P 500 over the same window.
  - These numbers flatter the strategy because the end point is an all-time high. At the 2 Mar 2026 close (151,973) the USD CAGR is lower still.
  - The FX rate at end-2025 is approximated from the "rupee about 280+" sources and should be checked against SBP data.
- **Real (inflation-adjusted) return:** Pakistan CPI averaged roughly high single to low double digits over 2008–2025, with a 38% peak. That suggests the **real PKR return is a fraction of the 14% nominal figure**, perhaps low-to-mid single digits. I could not source a cumulative CPI series in this session, so this needs PBS or SBP data before the report states it as fact.
- **Currency is the dominant risk.** In 2022–23 the index was flat in PKR but lost more than half its USD value. In FY24–25 the rupee was stable, so USD returns matched PKR returns. A local investor's real wealth depends on FX and inflation as much as on stock selection.
- **Policy-rate sensitivity:** PSX rallies track SBP easing cycles (22% to 10.5%). High risk-free rates (T-bills and money-market funds at 15–20%+ in 2023–24) are a serious competitor to equities. Equity is attractive mainly when real rates are falling.
- **Liquidity and governance:** MSCI's 2021 downgrade (only four FM-eligible names), the 2008 price floor and freeze, the March 2020 halt and the March 2026 circuit-breaker halt all show the **exit-liquidity and market-intervention risk** that Jhunjhunwala, Pabrai and Li Lu took on only with concentrated, long-horizon capital. Retail investors face this risk without diversification.
- **Concentration:** The KSE-100 is heavily weighted to banks, oil and gas exploration, fertiliser, cement and power. I could not source exact sector weights in this session. This means the index itself is a concentrated bet on the domestic interest-rate and energy cycle.
- **Applying the principles:**
  - Li Lu's "fish where the fish are" and the circle-of-competence idea favour understandable cash-generative domestic franchises.
  - Pabrai's margin of safety demands discounts large enough to cover currency and political risk.
  - Jhunjhunwala's lesson is that a multi-decade domestic consumption bet requires macro stability that India had and Pakistan has lacked.
  - The SPIVA evidence (70–80%+ of Indian active funds lag over 10 years) suggests a **low-cost index or ETF core plus currency diversification** is the base case. But Pakistani index products charge about 1–2.3%, which erodes much of the passive advantage.
