# Howard Marks (Oaktree), Nick Sleep & Qais Zakaria (Nomad), Terry Smith (Fundsmith): 21st-century records and methods (2000–Nov 2026)

Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Primary documents were read in full where possible: the Nomad 2001 and 2013 annual letters, the Fundsmith 2024 and 2025 annual letters, and Oaktree's memos "The Race to the Bottom" (2007), "The Limits to Negativism" (2008) and "Sea Change" (2022). Everything else is secondary and labelled as such.

## Q1. Howard Marks / Oaktree: 2008–09 distressed deployment, fund returns, key memos, principles, Brookfield deal

### Takeaway
Oaktree raised its largest-ever distressed fund in 2007–08: OCM Opportunities Fund VIIb, about $10.9bn committed, which started investing in May 2008. It invested roughly $450–500m+ a week in the ~15 weeks after Lehman (about $6–7bn by year-end 2008; the exact total differs by source). The fund's net IRR was reported at 31.5% early on (to end-2009). By end-2015 the SEC-filed figure had settled at about 16.7% net / 22% gross and 2.0x. Marks's memos show the method. "Race to the Bottom" (Feb 2007) warned about falling credit standards before the crisis. "The Limits to Negativism" (Oct 2008) set out the contrarian case for buying. "Sea Change" (Dec 2022) argued that credit had become attractive again. Brookfield bought 62% of Oaktree in 2019 (about $4.7bn) and agreed in Oct 2025 to buy the remaining ~26% for about $3bn.

### Cited Findings
**Fund VIIb size, timing, deployment**
- OCM Opportunities Fund VIIb: investment period May 2008 to May 2011; $10,940m committed and $9,844m drawn; distributions of about $17.3bn; gross IRR 22.0%, **net IRR 16.7%**, 2.0x multiple as of 31 Dec 2015 (Oaktree Capital Group FY2015 10-K closed-end fund table; the column mapping comes from a flattened extraction, so verify against the filing). The 10-K defines net IRR as returns to non-affiliated investors after management fees, expenses and the GP incentive allocation. — [Oaktree FY2015 10-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403528/000140352816000045/a201510-kdoc.htm)
- Earlier reported figure: "net IRR of 31.5% from inception through December 31, 2009", attributed to an unnamed investor rather than Oaktree. The same article describes the fund as a "$10.9 billion distressed vehicle raised in 2008", the largest ever per Preqin. Oaktree had $73bn AUM at end-2009, including $27bn distressed and $22bn high yield. Marks quote: a "bargain bonanza"; "We are unlikely to see the quantity or the vast bargains of late 2008." — [Institutional Investor, "Howard Marks: The Distressed-Debt King"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgc08ca1yjgus3sms5c/portfolio/howard-marks-the-distressed-debt-king)
- **Conflict:** 31.5% net IRR (end-2009, interim and unaudited by Oaktree) vs 16.7% net IRR (end-2015 SEC filing). The SEC figure is better supported as the near-final outcome. Interim IRRs on early-recovery marks were inflated by the quick 2009 rebound.
- The fund returned ~$3bn to LPs before its investment period ended, with NAV of ~$14bn at that point (2011). — [Buyouts Insider](https://www.buyoutsinsider.com/oaktree-pays-out-3b-from-10-9b-fund-viib/); [Private Funds CFO](https://www.privatefundscfo.com/print-editions/2011-03/oaktree-hands-back-3bn-from-2008-fund/)
- Deployment pace: Marks (in a Bloomberg Masters in Business interview, per search snippets) said Oaktree invested **over $500m a week from 15 Sept 2008 to year-end**, i.e. about 15 weeks. The Globe and Mail cites the same half-billion-a-week pace over the last 15 weeks of 2008. — [Ritholtz transcript](https://ritholtz.com/2018/10/transcript-howard-marks-oaktree-capital/) (page returned 403 on fetch; snippet only); [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/rob-magazine/invest-like-a-legend-howard-marks/article37716588/)
- **Conflict on the total deployed:** one blog says ~$6.5bn by year-end 2008, with the fund ~70% invested. A summary of a later podcast says $7bn in 15 weeks. Another account gives "$450 million a week". The fund was reported 90% invested by end-Q1 2009. — [BigGo summary](https://finance.biggo.com/news/98384518591a2204); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/07/why-howard-marks-calls-2008-his-proudest-investing-moment/). Best-supported statement: "~$450–500m+/week for ~15 weeks, roughly $6–7bn." The widely cited "$6bn+" is consistent with this, but no single primary Oaktree document stating an exact total was found.
- In "Sea Change", Marks says Oaktree raised its largest fund to that point in 2007–08 and was "putting most of it to work very successfully in the wake of the Lehman Brothers bankruptcy". He says the firm then spent 2009–2019 "in the wilderness" and "thought it appropriate... to cut the amount in half for its successor fund." — [Oaktree, "Sea Change", 13 Dec 2022](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change)
- MoneyWeek reports a 2008 restructuring position that made $2.2bn (a 23-fold return) and crisis-era investments making ~$9bn overall. These are not clearly tied to VIIb; treat with caution. — [MoneyWeek via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/money-week/20220520/282046215707570)

**"The Race to the Bottom" (14 Feb 2007), primary text**
- Mechanism: "It helps to think of money as a commodity just like those others... if you want to place more money... you have to make your money cheaper." Beyond accepting lower returns, the other thing providers can accept less of is "safety. So a provider of capital who wants to increase market share... will accept risks that others won't." — [Oaktree memo PDF](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-02-14-the-race-to-the-bottom.pdf)
- Examples in the memo: "The Amazing Disappearing Covenant", weakening covenants in sponsor-backed deals, and "toggle bonds" letting borrowers convert cash-pay bonds to PIK. — same source
- Conclusion: "there's a race to the bottom going on, reflecting a widespread reduction in the level of prudence on the part of investors and capital providers." Quoting Buffett: "The less prudence with which others conduct their affairs, the greater the prudence with which we should conduct our own affairs." On the cost of refusing: "you'll (a) lag in terms of return and (b) look like an old fogey." — same source

**"The Limits to Negativism" (15 Oct 2008), the buy rationale, primary text**
- "The last few weeks witnessed the greatest panic I've ever seen..." — [Oaktree memo PDF](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-10-15-the-limits-to-negativism.pdf)
- "Skepticism and pessimism aren't synonymous. Skepticism calls for pessimism when optimism is excessive. But it also calls for optimism when pessimism is excessive." — same
- "Sure, the negative story may turn out to be true, but certainly it's priced into the market... if it turns out not to be true, the appreciation from today's depressed levels will be enormous. I buy!" — same
- On the pendulum: "following the beliefs of the herd – and swinging with the pendulum – will give you average performance in the long run and can get you killed at the extremes." — same
- Macro reasoning: governments "will do everything they can... The philosophical bridge of unlimited aid to private institutions appears to have been crossed." — same

**"Sea Change" (13 Dec 2022), primary**
- Thesis: "We've gone from the low-return world of 2009-21 to a full-return world"; "Interest rates aren't about to decline by another 2,000 basis points from here"; base rate "more likely to average 2-4%". — [Oaktree](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change)
- Three sea changes: (1) the late-1970s acceptance of risk/return thinking, i.e. high yield becoming investable; (2) four decades of falling rates after Volcker; (3) "I think we may be in the midst of a third one today." — same
- Implication: "Investors can now potentially get solid returns from credit instruments"; "Lenders and bargain hunters face much better prospects... than they did in 2009-21"; "The investment strategies that worked best over those periods may not be the ones that outperform in the years ahead." — same; also [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/12/14/billionaire-howard-marks-oaktree-investing-markets-low-to-full-return-interest-rates-inflation)
- A follow-up memo, "Further Thoughts on Sea Change", was published Oct 2023. — [Seeking Alpha listing](https://seekingalpha.com/author/oaktree-capital/analysis?page=2)

**Brookfield acquisition**
- Announced 13 Mar 2019: Brookfield to acquire **62%** of Oaktree for ~**$4.7bn** ($49 cash or 1.077 BAM shares per unit, a ~12.4% premium). Combined AUM ~$475bn. Marks stayed as co-chairman, with Karsh as co-chairman/CIO and Wintrob as CEO; the firms operate independently; full ownership possible by 2029. Completed 30 Sep 2019. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/brookfield-buys-oaktree-to-build-an-alternatives-giant/78581); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-03-13/brookfield-agrees-to-acquire-a-majority-stake-in-oaktree-capital); [AltsWire](https://altswire.com/brookfield-buy-majority-stake-oaktree-capital-4-7-billion-deal/)
- 13 Oct 2025: Brookfield agreed to buy the remaining ~26% for ~**$3bn**, implying ~$11.5bn for 100% of Oaktree. — [Bloomberg Tax/BGov](https://news.bloombergtax.com/esg/brookfield-to-acquire-remaining-oaktree-stake-for-3-billion-1); [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/2025/10/13/oaktree-de-howard-marks-vende-fatia-restante-a-brookfield-por-us-3-bilhoes/)
- **Conflict on the closing date:** MarketScreener says 5 Mar 2026. Kalkine/Panabee say 31 Jul 2026, citing an 8-K. The 8-K-backed July date is better supported but was not verified directly. — [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/brookfield-corporation-tsx-bn-and-brookfield-asset-management-ltd-completed-the-acquisition-of-th-ce7e5fdbdd88f624); [Kalkine](https://kalkinemedia.com/us/news/announcements/brookfield-finalizes-3-billion-deal-for-complete-ownership-of-oaktree-capital-group)

### Inferences
- The 2007–08 sequence shows Marks's method end to end. He diagnosed a cycle peak from the behaviour of capital providers (covenant erosion, toggle PIKs) rather than from forecasts. He raised a record distressed fund while prices were still high but held commitments uncalled. Then he deployed fast when the "pendulum" hit maximum pessimism, framing the bet as an asymmetry ("priced in" downside vs enormous upside if the world did not end).
- VIIb's final net IRR (~16–17%) was far below the early 31.5% headline and below its 25–30% gross target. It was still a strong absolute result on ~$11bn, with roughly $17bn+ distributed on $9.8bn drawn. The deployment pace, not the IRR, is the more distinctive and repeatable lesson.

### Gaps
- No primary Oaktree document giving the exact dollar total deployed in the 15 weeks after Lehman was retrieved. The Ritholtz/Bloomberg transcript returned a 403.
- Final VIIb figures after 2015 were not found; later 10-Ks list mostly newer funds. Oaktree-wide composite returns (e.g., the distressed debt strategy's since-1988 gross/net IRR) were not retrieved.
- Verbatim second-level-thinking definition: the Oaktree "It's Not Easy" (2015) memo PDF could not be downloaded, and *The Most Important Thing* (2011) and *Mastering the Market Cycle* (2018) were not accessed directly. The well-known formulation (first-level thinking is "simplistic and superficial"; second-level is "deep, convoluted and complex") should be checked against the book or memo before quoting.

## Q2. Nomad Investment Partnership (Sleep & Zakaria, Sep 2001 – 2014): returns, scale economies shared, concentration, closure, inactivity, fees

### Takeaway
Per the final (2013) annual letter, Nomad returned **921.1% cumulative from 10 Sep 2001 to 31 Dec 2013, vs 116.9% for the MSCI World (net, US$)**. Annualised, that is **20.8% before performance fees vs 6.5%**, and **18.4% after performance fees**. The widely quoted 921% is therefore **pre-performance-fee** (after the small cost-reimbursement management fee), not net. The book started as a global deep-value portfolio (2001: heavily Asia, media, hotels). It evolved into a concentrated portfolio of "scale economies shared" compounders, chiefly Amazon, Costco and Berkshire. It used a refundable performance fee with a hurdle, and closed in 2014.

### Cited Findings
**Performance (primary: 2013 annual letter)**
- Since inception (10 Sep 2001) to 31 Dec 2013: Nomad **+921.1%** vs MSCI World (net) US$ **+116.9%**. Annualised: **20.8% before performance fees**, **18.4% after**, vs 6.5% for the index. "Value of a dollar invested at inception (pre-fees) $10.21 vs $2.17." — [Nomad 2013 Annual Letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)
- Net/gross definition, verbatim: "The figures above are unaudited, presented on a cumulative, percentage gain basis and, as ever, are before fees... Nomad's refundable performance fee and time dependent performance fee hurdle mean that the net-of-all-fees results will vary with subscription date". After fees: "Nomad has compounded at over eighteen percent per annum, whilst the index has managed an annual six and a half percent." — same
- Calendar years (Nomad vs MSCI World): 2001 (from 10 Sep) +10.1 / +3.6; 2002 +1.3 / −19.9; 2003 +79.6 / +33.1; 2004 +22.1 / +14.7; 2005 +9.2 / +9.5; 2006 +13.6 / +20.1; 2007 +21.2 / +9.0; **2008 −45.3 / −40.7**; 2009 +71.5 / +30.0; 2010 +43.9 / +11.8; 2011 −9.9 / −5.5; 2012 +39.8 / +15.8; 2013 +62.2 / +26.7. — same
- Underperformance periods: Nomad lagged the index in 2005 (marginally), 2006, 2008 and 2011. Six-year trailing to end-2013 (i.e. from end-2007) was +176.6% vs +19.4%. — same
- Audit: performance figures were produced by the administrator (Phoenix) and audited by Ernst & Young to 2011, then by Grant Thornton. — same
- **Conflict in secondary sources:** some call 921% "after performance fees" or "net". Per the letter, that is wrong; 921.1% and 20.8% are pre-performance-fee. — [Equity Mates](https://equitymates.com/company/nomad-partnership/), [Everything Money](https://www.everythingmoney.com/blog/nick-sleep-the-greatest-investor-you-ve-never-heard-of-292) (secondary; contradicted by the primary letter above)

**Fees**
- Management fee was cost-reimbursement only: "Nomad's (cost reimbursement) management fee remains around ten basis points per annum." — [Nomad 2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf). 2006 letter: "As the Partnership grows in size the management fee will decline as a percentage of assets." — [Massive Moats summary](https://massivemoats.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-analysis-into-the-nomad)
- Performance fee: the letter confirms it was "refundable" with a "time dependent performance fee hurdle". Secondary sources say it was **20% of returns above a 6% annual hurdle**, held in a reserve account and at risk (clawback) for years; one says five years. These exact terms are not confirmed from a primary document. — [2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf); secondary: search summaries of [Equity Mates](https://equitymates.com/episode/em-chat-nomad-partnership-letters/) and Substack summaries ([Torre Financial](https://torrefinancial.substack.com/p/the-nomad-investment-partnership))

**Early approach (primary: Jan 2002 letter on 2001)**
- Launched early Sep 2001 and began investing 10 Sep 2001, the day before 9/11: "The price of businesses fell almost immediately... we were able to invest a sizeable portion of the fund much faster than we would have anticipated." 18 investments; media ~21%, hotels/resorts/casinos 12%, telecom 10%; geography SE Asia 32.8%, North America 23.6%, Europe 12.7%, other EM 2.9%; ~28% cash at year-end. — [Nomad 2001 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e8204e94728ecc4b13995_2001%20Nomad%20Interim%20Letter.pdf)
- Criteria: "businesses trading at around half of their real business value, companies run by owner-oriented management and employing capital allocation strategies consistent with long term shareholder wealth creation." Examples: International Speedway (3.7%) and Matichon, a Thai newspaper (3.2%, ~4x normalised FCF, 9% yield). — same
- Structure: Marathon Asset Management was GP from 2001 to Sep 2006; Sleep, Zakaria & Co. after that. — [2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)

**Scale economies shared, concentration, inactivity (letters as quoted by a secondary deep-dive)**
- "Most companies pursue scale efficiencies, but few share them." (2004); "the company grows through giving more back." (2004); "Costco's obsession with sharing scale benefits with the customer" (2005); "Scale economics works well in bad economic times as well as good." (2008). — [Massive Moats](https://massivemoats.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-analysis-into-the-nomad)
- Amazon sizing: "one sixth of the Partnership invested in a volatile stock, such as Amazon" (2007). Amazon reportedly reached ~40% of invested capital at one point (2011, secondary claim). — same
- Inactivity: "The research continues but, as far as purchase or sale transactions in Nomad are concerned, we are inactive." / "the decision not to do something is still an active decision" (2012). — same
- Holding periods (primary): "we own shares for such long periods... that we write around twenty letters during the life of the average investment", i.e. ~10-year average holding implied. — [2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)
- Attribution (primary): returns are "the capitalisation of the success of the firms in which we have invested (minus our fees!)". The letter names "Messrs. Bezos, Robertson, Fernandes, Sinegal, Buffett, Steiner", i.e. Amazon, Air Asia (Fernandes), Costco (Sinegal), Berkshire (Buffett), plus Robertson and Steiner, likely Stagecoach-era and Weight Watchers/other holdings (not specified). — same
- Mistakes: Stagecoach ("This error was reinforced by misjudgments such as denial... and ego", 2007); Monsanto sold ~9 months after inception after an analytical error; "the biggest error an investor can make is the sale of a Wal-Mart or a Microsoft" (2007). — [Massive Moats](https://massivemoats.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-analysis-into-the-nomad)
- Zimbabwe (primary, 2013): mid-2000s purchases of a cement company, a brewery and an engineering firm via Jo'burg/Harare dual listings. Through hyperinflation and market suspension they were valued at zero, then exited in 2013 at "three and eight-fold in US dollars"; "not one we would repeat in a hurry". — [2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)

**Closure**
- The 2013 letter was the "twenty-fifth letter to investors over fourteen calendar years". It devotes much space to the heavier FCA/AIFMD/FATCA/SEC regulatory burden: "the regulatory landscape is not supportive of small, simple investment boutiques". — [2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)
- The partnership was wound up and capital returned in 2014. Sleep and Zakaria kept their own money concentrated, reportedly mostly in Amazon, Costco and Berkshire. Later secondary claims: Zakaria's Amazon stake ~70% of his assets; Sleep sold half his Amazon in 2018; about $2bn of gains generated for partners. — [Massive Moats](https://massivemoats.substack.com/p/a-deep-dive-analysis-into-the-nomad); [Equity Mates](https://equitymates.com/episode/em-chat-nomad-partnership-letters/)

### Inferences
- The record splits into two phases. In 2001–03 it was a deep-value, EM-heavy, half-of-value book; 2002–03 returned +1.3% and +79.6% against a falling index. From the mid-2000s it was a concentrated "scale economies shared" franchise book, which delivered 2009–2013 (+404% over five years). The 2008 drawdown (−45%) was worse than the index. Concentration produced high volatility, which they explicitly accepted.
- The pre-fee vs after-fee distinction matters: about 2.4 percentage points a year (20.8% vs 18.4%). Any report should say "921% before performance fees; ~18.4% p.a. net since inception".

### Gaps
- Exact performance-fee percentage, hurdle mechanics and clawback period were not confirmed from a primary letter (the 2001 letter retrieved did not state them). Peak AUM was not found in reliable sources; figures of ~$3–4bn circulate but are unverified.
- Exact weights of Amazon/Costco/Berkshire at closure, Amazon's purchase date and cost basis (circa 2005–06 is commonly cited), and the exact 2014 return-of-capital date and terms were not verified from primary sources. A 2014 closing letter text was not retrieved.
- The published "Full Collection of Letters" compilation was not accessed as one document; individual letters were.

## Q3. Fundsmith Equity Fund (Terry Smith, Nov 2010 – present): returns, strategy, 2021–2026 underperformance, outflows, Smith's explanations and the 2026 strategy shift

### Takeaway
From 1 Nov 2010 to 31 Dec 2025 the fund (T Class Acc, **net of fees**, GBP) returned **+612.9% cumulative / 13.8% annualised, vs MSCI World (£ net) +467.6% / 12.1%**. It has underperformed MSCI World for **five consecutive calendar years (2021–2025)**; 2025 was +0.8% vs +12.8%. In H1 2026 it fell −2.9% vs +11.2%, cutting cumulative outperformance to 593.5% vs 526.2% (to ~8 Jul 2026). AUM fell from a ~£29bn peak to ~£12bn. Smith blames index concentration (the Magnificent Seven/Nvidia/AI), passive flows and dollar weakness. In H1 2026 he made a large break from "do nothing", with 51.8% turnover, 12 buys and 13 exits, and a new emphasis on momentum.

### Cited Findings
**Headline numbers (primary letters)**
- 2025: Fund **+0.8%** vs MSCI World **+12.8%**; IA Global +10.8%. Since inception to 31 Dec 2025: **+612.9% vs +467.6%** cumulative; **+13.8% vs +12.1%** annualised. Sortino 0.75 vs 0.48. T Class Acc, net of fees, noon UK pricing; MSCI World £ net at US close. "The Fund is not managed with reference to any benchmark." **Strategy and how it was executed (primary 2025 letter)**
- "We continue to apply a simple three step investment strategy: Buy good companies; Don't overpay; Do nothing." — [2025 letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/4hcfd1pg/2025-fef-annual-letter-web.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cumulative outperformance since inception has shrunk sharply. At end-2024 the fund led the index by 204 percentage points cumulative (607 vs 403); by mid-2026 the lead was ~67 points (594 vs 526). Annualised alpha fell from about +2.7pp to about +1.7pp at end-2025, and lower still by mid-2026. Most of the fund's lifetime excess was earned in 2011–2020.
- Several 2025 purchases were sold within months in H1 2026: Zoetis, EssilorLuxottica, Intuit and Wolters Kluwer, along with Magnum. That, the turnover jump from 3.2% to 51.8%, and the explicit adoption of momentum amount to a material departure from "do nothing". It bears directly on any claim that the strategy has been unchanged since 2010.
- Underperformance drivers are a mix of factors. Some are structural (no Nvidia, Apple, Amazon or Tesla; a consumer-staples and healthcare tilt). Some are stock-specific (Novo Nordisk, Diageo/Brown-Forman/PepsiCo in staples) or currency-driven (USD weakness hits a GBP fund with mostly US holdings).
